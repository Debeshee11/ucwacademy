Key terms, examples and exam traps

Interest rate futures

An interest rate future is an exchange-traded contract on an underlying interest rate instrument, such as a government security or T-bill.

Example. A fund buys a G-Sec future on the exchange.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Interest rate futures trade only over the counter.

Notional underlying

Interest rate futures on bonds use a notional security, so settlement is in cash and no actual bond is delivered.

Example. A contract settles in cash against a notional bond.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The seller must deliver a bond at expiry.

Price and yield

The futures price moves inversely to yield, so a rise in yields lowers the futures price.

Example. A long position loses if yields rise.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A long position gains when yields rise.

Long and short IRF

A long position gains when yields fall and a short position gains when yields rise.

Example. A bond fund sells futures to hedge against a rate rise.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A short position gains when yields fall.

PV01

PV01 is the change in the value of a position for a one basis point change in yield.

Example. A position loses ₹5,000 for each 1 bp rise in yield.

Exam trap. This statement is false: PV01 is measured for a 1% change in price.

Basis point

One basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point, so 100 basis points equal 1%.

Example. A rise from 7.00% to 7.25% is 25 basis points.

Exam trap. This statement is false: One basis point equals one percent.

Hedge ratio

The hedge ratio gives the number of futures contracts needed to offset the portfolio's interest rate sensitivity, using the PV01 of the portfolio and the future.

Example. A fund divides its portfolio PV01 by the future's PV01.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The hedge ratio is the portfolio's credit rating.

T-bill futures

A T-bill future is based on a short-term treasury bill and can be used to hedge short-term rates.

Example. A short-term fund hedges using T-bill futures.

Exam trap. This statement is false: T-bill futures are based on long-term bonds.

Mark-to-market in IRF

Interest rate futures are marked to market daily like other futures, with gains and losses settled through margin.

Example. A day's yield rise is settled in the margin account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Interest rate futures are settled only at expiry.

Interest rate options

An interest rate option gives the buyer the right, not the obligation, to buy or sell the underlying interest rate instrument at a strike.

Example. A buyer pays premium to protect against a yield rise.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An interest rate option obliges the buyer to transact.

Basis risk in IRF

Hedging with futures leaves basis risk because the portfolio and the contract do not move together perfectly.

Example. A hedge does not offset all loss because bonds differ.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A futures hedge removes every kind of risk.

Who uses IRDs

Banks, mutual funds, insurers and corporates use interest rate derivatives to manage rate risk.

Example. A debt fund manager uses futures to manage duration.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Interest rate derivatives are only for speculators.
