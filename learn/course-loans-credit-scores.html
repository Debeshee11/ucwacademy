Key ideas you will take away

  • EMI = principal + interest.
  • Longer tenure lowers EMI, raises cost.
  • Floating rates move with the benchmark.
  • Fixed rates stay fixed for a period.
  • Prepay early for most saving.
  • No penalty on floating-rate individual loans.

Try a question

Q1. Which statement best describes “Principal”?

  1. A fixed rate stays the same for an agreed period, so your EMI is predictable even if market rates move.
  2. Prepayment is paying part or all of a loan ahead of schedule. It reduces the outstanding principal and the interest you pay.
  3. Revolving credit is credit you can use, repay and use again up to a limit, such as a credit card. Unpaid balances attract interest.
  4. The principal is the original amount you borrow. Interest is calculated on the outstanding principal.
Show the answer

D. Principal: The principal is the original amount you borrow. Interest is calculated on the outstanding principal.

Q2. Which statement best describes “Fixed rate”?

  1. Credit utilisation is the share of your credit limit that you are using. Keeping it low, often below 30%, helps your score.
  2. A fixed rate stays the same for an agreed period, so your EMI is predictable even if market rates move.
  3. A processing fee is a one-time charge by the lender when sanctioning a loan, usually a percentage of the loan amount.
  4. The interest rate is the cost of borrowing, shown as a yearly percentage of the outstanding loan.
Show the answer

B. Fixed rate: A fixed rate stays the same for an agreed period, so your EMI is predictable even if market rates move.
