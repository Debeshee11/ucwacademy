Key ideas you will take away

  • Four parts: principal, rate, tenure, EMI. Never judge by EMI alone.
  • Total repaid = EMI × months. Ask for it every time.
  • Fixed: certain EMI, usually a higher rate. Floating: moves with the benchmark.
  • Rate up means higher EMI or longer tenure. You can choose.
  • Prepay early: that is when it saves the most.
  • Keep the EMI, shorten the tenure.

Try a question

Q1. A loan has an EMI of ₹15,000 for 60 months. The amount borrowed was ₹7 lakh. How much interest is paid over the loan?

  1. ₹1 lakh
  2. ₹2 lakh
  3. ₹7 lakh
  4. ₹9 lakh
Show the answer

B. Total repaid is ₹15,000 × 60 = ₹9 lakh. Subtract the ₹7 lakh borrowed and the interest is ₹2 lakh.

Q2. The repo rate rises by 0.5%. Prakash has a repo-linked floating-rate home loan. What should he expect?

  1. Nothing changes until the loan is renewed
  2. His rate rises at the next reset, lifting his EMI or his tenure
  3. His EMI falls because banks pass on the benefit
  4. His loan automatically converts to a fixed rate until the repo rate falls
Show the answer

B. A repo-linked rate is the repo rate plus a spread, so it moves when the repo rate moves. The lender then raises the EMI, extends the tenure, or both.
