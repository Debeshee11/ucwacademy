Key terms, examples and exam traps

Private placement memorandum

The PPM is the principal disclosure document describing the fund's strategy, terms, fees, risks and governance.

Example. A prospective investor reads the PPM before signing.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The PPM is signed by the investor as a contract.

Contribution agreement

The contribution (or subscription) agreement is the contract in which the investor commits capital to the fund on set terms.

Example. An investor signs to commit ₹3 crore under the fund's terms.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A contribution agreement is a loan agreement.

Trust deed

The trust deed sets up the fund as a trust, defining the roles of trustee, sponsor and manager, and the rights of beneficiaries.

Example. A legal document creates the trust and sets its rules.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The trust deed is a marketing brochure.

Investment management agreement

The IMA appoints the manager and sets its duties, authority, fees and standards of care.

Example. A contract between the trustee and the investment manager.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The IMA is an agreement among investors only.

Side letter

A side letter gives an individual investor special rights or terms, such as reporting or fee discounts, separate from the main documents.

Example. A large investor negotiates a lower fee in a separate letter.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A side letter changes terms for all investors.

Most-favoured-nation clause

An MFN clause lets an investor elect the better terms granted to other investors in comparable side letters.

Example. An investor opts for a lower fee granted to another investor.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An MFN clause gives the manager the best terms.

Default provisions

If an investor fails to meet a capital call, the fund can impose penalties, such as forfeiting part of the interest or reducing its holding.

Example. A defaulting investor loses part of its stake.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Missing a capital call has no consequences.

No-fault removal

A no-fault removal clause lets investors remove the manager by a supermajority vote without proving misconduct.

Example. Investors vote to replace the manager though no wrongdoing is alleged.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The manager can be removed only for fraud.

Indemnification

Indemnification clauses protect the manager from liability for acts in good faith but should exclude fraud, gross negligence and wilful default.

Example. The manager is protected unless it acts fraudulently.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The manager is indemnified for fraud.

Distribution provisions

These set how and when proceeds are distributed, including the waterfall, reinvestment rights and in-specie distributions.

Example. The fund pays investors in cash or in shares as per terms.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Distributions are at the manager's whim.

Term and extension

The term clause states the fund's life and the conditions under which it can be extended, normally needing investor approval.

Example. A fund can extend by two years with the vote of investors.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The manager may extend the term at any time.

Investor transfer rights

The documents set conditions for transferring units, usually needing manager consent, as the fund is illiquid.

Example. An investor needs approval to sell her units.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Units can be freely traded on exchanges.

Negotiating priorities

Investors, especially large ones, negotiate on fees, governance rights, reporting, key-person triggers and transfer terms.

Example. An anchor investor seeks a seat on the advisory committee.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Investor terms cannot be negotiated.

Governing law and disputes

The documents specify governing law, jurisdiction and dispute resolution, often through arbitration.

Example. Disputes go to arbitration in India as per the agreement.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Documents are silent on disputes.

Disclosures of risk factors

The PPM must list material risks; investors should read them and not rely on verbal assurances.

Example. An investor relies on the written risk factors.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Verbal assurances override written documents.
