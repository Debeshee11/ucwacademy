Key terms, examples and exam traps

Foreign portfolio investor

A foreign portfolio investor (FPI) is a registered foreign investor who invests in Indian securities through a demat account with a DP, and who must be registered through a designated depository participant.

Example. A foreign fund registers and opens a demat account to invest in listed shares.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An FPI may invest without registration or a demat account.

FPI investment limits

A single FPI can hold up to 10% of the paid-up capital of a company, and the combined holding of all FPIs is limited to 24%, which can be raised up to the sectoral cap.

Example. A fund keeps its stake in a company below 10%.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An FPI may hold any proportion of a company.

Basic services demat account

A basic services demat account (BSDA) is a low-cost demat account for small investors with limited holdings, and an investor can hold only one BSDA.

Example. A small investor opens a BSDA to hold a few shares.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An investor may hold several BSDAs across depositories.

BSDA eligibility

A BSDA is meant for an individual with only one demat account in her name, and the value of securities in it must not exceed ₹10 lakh.

Example. An investor with holdings of ₹6 lakh qualifies for a BSDA.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A BSDA is available for holdings of any value.

BSDA charges

For a BSDA, the annual maintenance charge is nil for holdings up to ₹4 lakh and up to ₹100 plus GST for holdings above ₹4 lakh and up to ₹10 lakh.

Example. An account with ₹3 lakh pays no annual maintenance charge.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A BSDA pays the same annual maintenance charge as any other account.
