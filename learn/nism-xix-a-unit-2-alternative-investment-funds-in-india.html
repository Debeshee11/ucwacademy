Key terms, examples and exam traps

Alternative Investment Fund

An AIF is a privately pooled investment vehicle that raises money from investors for investing as per a defined policy, regulated by SEBI.

Example. A trust pools ₹100 crore from wealthy investors for private investments.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An AIF raises money from the general public.

Category I AIF

Category I AIFs invest in start-ups, early-stage ventures, SMEs, social ventures and infrastructure that the government considers socially or economically desirable.

Example. A venture capital fund backing start-ups.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Category I AIFs use high leverage.

Category II AIF

Category II AIFs, such as private equity and debt funds, do not undertake leverage except for day-to-day operational needs and are not given specific incentives.

Example. A private equity fund investing in unlisted companies.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Category II AIFs are hedge funds trading with leverage.

Category III AIF

Category III AIFs, such as hedge funds, employ diverse or complex trading strategies and may use leverage and trade listed derivatives.

Example. A long-short equity fund using derivatives.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Category III AIFs cannot use leverage.

Angel fund

An angel fund is a sub-category of Category I that pools money from angel investors to invest in early-stage start-ups, with smaller minimums.

Example. An angel network invests in seed-stage ventures.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An angel fund invests in listed blue-chips.

Minimum investment

Each investor in an AIF must invest at least ₹1 crore, with a lower limit of ₹25 lakh for angel funds and employees or directors of the AIF's manager.

Example. An investor needs to commit at least ₹1 crore.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The minimum investment is ₹10,000.

Minimum corpus

An AIF scheme must have a corpus of at least ₹20 crore (₹5 crore for an angel fund) before it begins investing.

Example. A fund must raise ₹20 crore to begin.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An AIF can start with any amount.

Number of investors

An AIF scheme cannot have more than 1,000 investors (200 for angel funds) as it raises money through private placement.

Example. A scheme caps its investor count at 1,000.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An AIF can have unlimited investors.

Accredited investor

An accredited investor is one who meets income or net worth criteria and is recognised for certain relaxations in the AIF framework.

Example. A highly wealthy investor is certified with an accreditation agency.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An accredited investor is a retail depositor.
