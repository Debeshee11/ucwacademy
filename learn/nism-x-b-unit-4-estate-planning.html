Key terms, examples and exam traps

Estate planning

Estate planning arranges how assets will pass to the next generation, minimising disputes, delay and cost.

Example. A client documents her wishes while alive.

Exam trap. This statement is false: It begins only after death.

Will

A will is a legal declaration of how a person wants her property distributed after death, and it takes effect only on death.

Example. A testator names her heirs in a signed will.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A will operates from the date of signing.

Testator and executor

The testator makes the will and the executor is the person appointed to carry it out.

Example. A friend named to execute the will.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The executor makes the will.

Valid will

A will must be in writing, signed by the testator and attested by two witnesses, and registration is optional.

Example. A will signed before two witnesses.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Registration is compulsory.

Revoking a will

A will can be changed or revoked any time before death, and a later will overrides the earlier one.

Example. A client writes a new will after marriage.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A will cannot be changed after signing.

Intestate succession

If a person dies without a will, the property passes according to personal succession laws.

Example. A man dies without a will and the law decides.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The state takes the property.

Hindu Succession Act

This Act governs intestate succession among Hindus, with equal rights for sons and daughters in coparcenary property.

Example. A daughter inherits equally.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Daughters have no rights under the Act.

Indian Succession Act

This Act governs wills and succession for Christians and others, and the rules for probate of wills.

Example. A will's probate rules.

Exam trap. This statement is false: It applies only to Hindus.

Muslim personal law

Muslims follow personal law for succession, with defined shares, and a will is limited to a share of the estate.

Example. A Muslim testator is limited in the portion she can will away.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A Muslim may will away the whole estate freely.

Probate

Probate is the court's certification that a will is genuine and gives the executor authority to act.

Example. An executor obtains it to transfer assets.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Probate is a tax on the estate.

Nomination

A nominee receives assets on the holder's death but may hold them for the legal heirs, depending on the law that applies.

Example. A bank pays a nominee and the nominee holds it for heirs.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A nominee overrides the will in all cases.

Legal heirs

Legal heirs are the persons entitled under succession law or a will, and they can claim assets irrespective of nomination in many cases.

Example. Heirs may claim the share from the nominee.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Legal heirs have no claim if a nominee exists.

Succession certificate

A succession certificate lets the heirs of a person who died without a will collect debts and securities.

Example. Heirs obtain it to claim bank dues.

Exam trap. This statement is false: It is required for all wills.

Trust

A trust holds property for the benefit of others under a trustee, allowing control, privacy and protection.

Example. A settlor creates a trust for her children.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A trust is a type of will.

Settlor, trustee, beneficiary

The settlor creates the trust, the trustee manages it and the beneficiary gets the benefits.

Example. The trustee manages assets for the beneficiary.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The beneficiary manages the trust.

Revocable and irrevocable trust

A revocable trust can be changed or ended by the settlor, while an irrevocable trust cannot, giving stronger separation of assets.

Example. A settlor retains the power to cancel the trust.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An irrevocable trust can be withdrawn easily.

Private trust

A private trust may be set up for the family to hold assets, manage inheritance and protect minors or dependants.

Example. A family trust holds a business stake.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A private trust must be registered as a charity.

Power of attorney

A power of attorney authorises another person to act for the principal during life and ends on the principal's death.

Example. An agent signs documents when the owner is abroad.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A power of attorney continues after death.

Gifting

Gifting during life transfers ownership immediately via a gift deed, and property gifts require registration.

Example. A parent gifts a flat to a child by registered deed.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An oral gift of property is valid.

Inheritance tax

India has no inheritance or estate tax at present, but income from inherited assets is taxable to the heir.

Example. An heir pays tax on rent from an inherited flat.

Exam trap. This statement is false: India taxes the transfer on inheritance.

Cost of inherited assets

For capital gains, the cost of an inherited asset is the cost to the previous owner and the holding period includes the previous holder's period.

Example. An heir sells an inherited house.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The cost is the value on the date of death only.

Joint holding

In a joint account with 'either or survivor', the surviving holder operates the account on the death of the other.

Example. A spouse continues to access the account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Joint accounts are frozen on any death.

Guardian for minors

A will can name a guardian for minor children, and a trust can manage their assets until adulthood.

Example. A parent appoints a guardian in the will.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Minors can hold and sell property directly.

Family settlement

A family settlement is an arrangement among family members to divide property and avoid disputes.

Example. Siblings agree on the division of assets in writing.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A family settlement needs no document.

Review of estate plan

The plan should be updated after marriage, birth, divorce, death or major changes in assets.

Example. A client updates the will after a new child.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An estate plan never needs revision.
