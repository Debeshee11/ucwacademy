Key terms, examples and exam traps

Depository participant

A depository participant (DP) is an agent of the depository through which investors access depository services, such as opening BO accounts.

Example. A bank branch acts as a DP and opens demat accounts for its customers.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A DP is an agent of the investor and not of the depository.

Eligibility to be a DP

Entities such as banks, public financial institutions, custodians, clearing corporations, stock brokers, NBFCs and registrars can become DPs, subject to net worth norms and approval.

Example. A SEBI-registered stock broker applies to become a DP.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Any individual can become a DP without registration.

DP registration

A DP must be registered with SEBI and admitted by the depository, and must meet net worth and other requirements.

Example. A stock broker obtains SEBI registration before acting as a DP.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A DP is registered with the depository only.

Depository and DP agreement

The DP enters into an agreement with the depository, and the BO enters into an agreement with the DP when opening an account.

Example. A new account holder signs account-opening documents with the DP.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The BO deals directly with the depository and signs nothing with the DP.

Issuer

An issuer is the company or body that issues securities and may offer them in demat form through the depository.

Example. A listed company makes its shares available in dematerialised form.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The issuer holds securities of BOs.

Registrar and transfer agent

A registrar and transfer agent maintains the issuer's records and handles share transfers and demat requests for the issuer.

Example. The registrar confirms a demat request and updates the issuer's records.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The registrar opens demat accounts for investors.

Clearing corporation and clearing member

The clearing corporation settles exchange trades, and clearing members hold accounts with it to settle obligations on behalf of brokers and clients.

Example. A clearing member delivers shares to the clearing corporation on the settlement day.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The clearing corporation deals directly with each investor.

Inspection and supervision

Depositories inspect DPs, monitor compliance and can take action for violations, and SEBI oversees the system.

Example. The depository audits a DP's records and procedures.

Exam trap. This statement is false: DPs are not subject to inspection.

Segregation of client assets

A DP must keep investors' securities separate from its own and cannot use client securities for its own benefit.

Example. A broker-DP keeps its own demat account separate from clients' accounts.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A DP may use clients' securities for its own dealings.
