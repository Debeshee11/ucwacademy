Key terms, examples and exam traps

Equity ownership

An equity share represents ownership in a company, with a residual claim on earnings and assets after creditors are paid.

Example. A shareholder is paid after lenders if a firm winds up.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Shareholders rank ahead of lenders.

Top-down analysis

Top-down analysis begins with the economy, moves to industries and then selects companies.

Example. A manager studies growth, then sectors, then stocks.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Top-down analysis starts with picking a single stock.

Bottom-up analysis

Bottom-up analysis starts with company-level research on business, management and financials, with less weight on the macro view.

Example. An analyst picks a firm on the strength of its balance sheet.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Bottom-up analysis ignores company financials.

Price-earnings ratio

The P/E ratio is the market price divided by earnings per share and shows what investors pay per rupee of earnings.

Example. A share at ₹200 with EPS of ₹10 has a P/E of 20.

Exam trap. This statement is false: P/E is price divided by book value.

Price-to-book ratio

P/B compares market price to book value per share and is often used for banks and asset-heavy firms.

Example. A bank trades at 2.5 times book value.

Exam trap. This statement is false: P/B compares price to dividends.

Return on equity

ROE is net profit divided by shareholders' equity and shows how well a firm earns on owners' capital.

Example. A firm earns ₹15 on every ₹100 of equity.

Exam trap. This statement is false: ROE measures return on total assets.

Free cash flow

Free cash flow is operating cash flow less capital expenditure, and is the cash available to capital providers.

Example. A firm generates cash after funding its capex.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Free cash flow equals reported net profit.

Discounted cash flow

DCF values a business as the present value of its expected future cash flows, discounted at a rate reflecting risk.

Example. An analyst discounts forecast cash flows at 12%.

Exam trap. This statement is false: DCF values a firm using past profits alone.

Technical analysis

Technical analysis studies price and volume patterns to time trades and assumes patterns tend to repeat.

Example. A trader uses moving averages to find a trend.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Technical analysis relies on the audited balance sheet.

Corporate governance

Good governance means a transparent, accountable board that protects minority shareholders, and weak governance is an investment risk.

Example. A manager avoids a firm with frequent related-party dealings.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Governance has no effect on valuation.

Stewardship

Institutional investors, including portfolio managers, are expected to monitor investee companies and vote in the interest of clients.

Example. A manager votes on a resolution after reviewing it.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Voting rights on client holdings can be ignored.
