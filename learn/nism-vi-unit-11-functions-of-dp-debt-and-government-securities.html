Key terms, examples and exam traps

Debt securities

Debt securities such as debentures and bonds are instruments by which an issuer borrows and pays interest, and can be held in demat form.

Example. An investor holds a corporate bond in her demat account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Debt securities represent ownership in the issuer.

Government securities

Government securities (G-secs) are debt instruments issued by the central or state governments, and they carry the lowest credit risk.

Example. A dated government security pays a fixed coupon every half-year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Government securities are issued by private companies.

Treasury bills

Treasury bills are short-term government securities issued at a discount and redeemed at face value, with maturities of 91, 182 and 364 days.

Example. An investor buys a 91-day bill below face value and receives face value on maturity.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Treasury bills pay a coupon every six months.

SGL account

A Subsidiary General Ledger (SGL) account with the RBI holds government securities in electronic form for eligible institutions.

Example. A bank holds its government securities in an SGL account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An SGL account is held by retail investors with a DP.

Constituent SGL

A Constituent SGL (CSGL) account lets a constituent hold government securities through a bank or institution that has an SGL account.

Example. A small institution holds government securities through its bank's SGL facility.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A CSGL account is the same as a demat account with CDSL.

Retail access to G-secs

Retail investors can invest in government securities through demat accounts with a DP or through the RBI Retail Direct facility.

Example. A retail investor buys a T-bill online.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Retail investors are barred from holding government securities.

Commercial paper and debt in demat

Money market instruments such as commercial paper are issued in dematerialised form.

Example. A company's commercial paper is credited to the holder's demat account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Commercial paper may be issued only in paper form.

Coupon and maturity

A debt security pays periodic interest, the coupon, and returns the face value on maturity, handled as a corporate action by the depository.

Example. On maturity the redemption proceeds are paid to the holder.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A debt security never repays the principal.

Settlement of debt trades

Debt trades are settled through clearing corporations, with securities moved between demat accounts on a delivery versus payment basis.

Example. Securities and cash change hands together on settlement.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Debt trades are settled by physical delivery.
