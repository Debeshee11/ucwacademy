Key terms, examples and exam traps

Portfolio management agreement

The agreement between manager and client sets out services, fees, rights, risks, and termination terms, and is mandatory.

Example. A client signs a detailed contract before investing.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A PMS can start without a written agreement.

Disclosure document

The disclosure document gives the client full information about the manager, investment approach, fees, risks and litigation before signing.

Example. A client receives a document describing the manager and charges before investing.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The disclosure document is given after the first year.

Fixed fee

A fixed management fee is charged as a percentage of assets irrespective of performance.

Example. A manager charges 2% of assets every year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A fixed fee depends on profits.

Return-based fee

A return-based fee is charged only on profits above a hurdle rate, subject to a high-water mark.

Example. The manager earns a share only of returns above 10%.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A return-based fee is charged even if the portfolio loses money.

Hybrid fee

A hybrid fee combines a lower fixed fee with a performance fee.

Example. A manager charges 1% of assets plus 10% of profits above a hurdle.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A hybrid fee has no fixed component.

Hurdle rate

A hurdle rate is the minimum return the portfolio must earn before a performance fee is charged.

Example. The manager earns a fee only when returns exceed 8%.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A hurdle rate is the maximum fee permitted.

High-water mark

The high-water mark is the highest value of the portfolio on which a performance fee was last paid; a new fee is charged only on gains above it.

Example. After a loss, no performance fee is charged until the value exceeds the previous peak.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A high-water mark resets every month.

Exit load

Exit load is a charge on withdrawal in the early years: at most 3% in the first year, 2% in the second and 1% in the third, with none thereafter.

Example. A client who exits early pays a decreasing charge.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Exit load is the same in every year.

Custodian

A custodian safekeeps the securities of PMS clients independent of the manager, and settles trades.

Example. An independent institution holds client assets.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The portfolio manager acts as the sole custodian.

Client reporting

Managers must give clients periodic reports showing portfolio holdings, transactions, performance and charges.

Example. A client receives a quarterly report on holdings and returns.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Managers need not report to clients.

Contribution of securities

Clients may contribute securities instead of cash, and the manager may hold them or realign the portfolio, with tax consequences.

Example. A client transfers existing shares to start a PMS.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Clients can contribute only cash.

Termination

Either party may terminate the agreement as per its terms, usually with notice; assets are returned in the form specified.

Example. A client withdraws from the service as the contract allows.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A client cannot leave the PMS before 10 years.

Withdrawals and partial redemptions

Clients may withdraw funds or securities as per the agreement, with notice and exit load as applicable.

Example. A client takes out part of the portfolio and bears any exit load.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Partial withdrawals are never allowed.

Brokerage and expenses

Brokerage and other expenses on transactions, such as custodian and audit fees, are charged to the client as disclosed.

Example. A client's statement shows brokerage and custodian charges.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Costs are not passed to PMS clients.

Audit and compliance

Portfolio managers are subject to annual audits and reporting to SEBI, and must have compliance officers.

Example. A firm's compliance is audited each year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Portfolio managers are never audited.

Conflict of interest

Managers must avoid and disclose conflicts, such as trading in the same securities for own account ahead of clients.

Example. A manager discloses related-party trades.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Conflicts need not be disclosed.
