Key terms, examples and exam traps

Heads of income

Income is classified as salary, house property, business or profession, capital gains and other sources.

Example. Rent is taxed under house property.

Exam trap. This statement is false: All income is taxed under one head.

Residential status

Tax liability depends on residential status; residents are taxed on global income and non-residents on India-sourced income.

Example. An NRI is taxed on income earned in India.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Residents are taxed only on Indian income.

Old and new regime

Individuals can choose between the old regime with deductions and the new regime with lower slab rates and few deductions.

Example. A client with large deductions compares both.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Deductions like 80C apply under the new regime.

Section 80C

Section 80C allows a deduction up to ₹1.5 lakh for items such as PPF, ELSS, life insurance premiums and EPF in the old regime.

Example. A client invests in ELSS for deduction.

Exam trap. This statement is false: 80C allows unlimited deduction.

ELSS

Equity Linked Savings Schemes are tax-saving mutual funds with a three-year lock-in.

Example. A client chooses ELSS under 80C.

Exam trap. This statement is false: ELSS has a 15-year lock-in.

Section 80D

Section 80D allows a deduction for health insurance premiums, with higher limits for senior citizens, in the old regime.

Example. A client claims the premium paid for her parents.

Exam trap. This statement is false: 80D covers life insurance premiums.

Section 80CCD(1B)

An additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 is available for NPS contributions, over and above 80C, in the old regime.

Example. A client adds to NPS for extra deduction.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The extra deduction is ₹5 lakh.

Standard deduction

Salaried individuals get a flat standard deduction from salary income, and the amount differs by regime.

Example. A salaried person gets it without any proof.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The standard deduction needs bills.

Short-term capital gains on equity

Gains on listed equity shares and equity funds held up to 12 months are short term and taxed at a flat rate, currently 20%.

Example. A fund sold after 8 months.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Short-term gains on equity are taxed at 15% always.

Long-term capital gains on equity

Gains on listed equity held for more than 12 months are taxed at 12.5% on the amount above the annual exemption of ₹1.25 lakh.

Example. A holder sells after two years.

Exam trap. This statement is false: LTCG on equity is fully exempt.

Debt mutual funds

Debt funds bought after April 2023 are taxed on gains at the investor's slab rate, whatever the holding period.

Example. A debt fund gain is added to income.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Debt funds get indexation benefit.

Holding period for other assets

Unlisted shares and immovable property need two years to be long term, and long-term gains are taxed at 12.5% without indexation under current rules.

Example. A flat sold after 3 years.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Property is long term after one year.

Gold

Gold is long term after 24 months, with gains taxed at 12.5% under current rules, while gold funds follow their own rules.

Example. Physical gold sold after 3 years.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Gold is long term after one year.

Set-off and carry forward

Capital losses can be set off against capital gains and carried forward for eight years, but long-term losses only against long-term gains.

Example. A short-term loss reduces long-term gain.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Long-term loss offsets any income.

Dividend taxation

Dividends are taxed in the hands of the recipient at the slab rate.

Example. Dividend income is added to total income.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Dividends are tax-free for all investors.

Interest income

Interest from bank deposits is taxable at slab rates, with TDS above specified limits.

Example. FD interest is added to income.

Exam trap. This statement is false: FD interest is tax-free.

TDS

Tax deducted at source is a prepayment of tax by the payer and can be adjusted in the return.

Example. A bank deducts tax on interest.

Exam trap. This statement is false: TDS is a separate additional tax.

Advance tax

Taxpayers expecting liability above a threshold pay advance tax in instalments through the year.

Example. A business pays four times a year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Advance tax is paid after the year ends.

Rental income

Rent is taxed under house property after a standard deduction of 30% of net annual value, plus home loan interest deduction subject to limits.

Example. A landlord claims a 30% deduction.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Rent is exempt from tax.

Home loan benefits

Interest on a home loan is deductible within limits for a self-occupied house, with principal repayment qualifying under 80C in the old regime.

Example. A borrower claims interest up to the limit.

Exam trap. This statement is false: There is no tax benefit from a home loan.

HUF

A Hindu Undivided Family is taxed as a separate entity, which allows another set of exemptions and slabs.

Example. A family splits income between itself and an HUF.

Exam trap. This statement is false: HUF is not a separate taxpayer.

Clubbing of income

Income from assets transferred to a spouse or minor child without adequate consideration is clubbed with the transferor's income.

Example. A gift to a spouse that earns interest is taxed in the giver's hands.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Gifts to a spouse always shift the tax.

Gifts

Gifts from specified relatives are exempt, while gifts from others above a threshold are taxed as income of the recipient.

Example. A gift from a parent is exempt.

Exam trap. This statement is false: All gifts are taxed.

Tax harvesting

Booking long-term gains up to the exemption limit each year and re-investing can reduce future tax.

Example. An investor sells units to use the annual exemption.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Tax harvesting avoids all tax.

Tax planning versus evasion

Planning uses legal provisions to reduce tax, while evasion hides income or misreports and is illegal.

Example. A client uses deductions properly.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Hiding income is tax planning.
