NISM Series XXI-A syllabus, unit by unit

The exam covers 12 units. The percentage is the share of questions each unit usually carries, so spend your time in that proportion.

Unit 1 Investments7%

The language of investing: asset classes, risk, return and the time value of money. Foundation for every later unit.

  • Asset classes and risk-return.
  • Time value and compounding.
  • Inflation and real return.
Unit 2 Introduction to securities markets5%

The markets in which portfolio managers operate and the regulators who oversee them.

  • Primary and secondary markets.
  • Market participants and regulators.
Unit 3 Investing in stocks10%

How portfolio managers analyse and select equity investments: valuation, style and risk.

  • Fundamental ratios and valuation.
  • Growth versus value; large versus small.
  • Equity risks.
Unit 4 Investing in fixed income securities5%

Bonds and debt instruments in a portfolio: yield, duration and credit.

  • Bond features.
  • Yield and duration.
  • Credit and interest-rate risk.
Unit 5 Derivatives5%

Derivatives in portfolios: hedging and the risks of leverage.

  • Futures and options.
  • Hedging.
  • Leverage risk.
Unit 6 Collective investment vehicles5%

Mutual funds, ETFs and AIFs compared with PMS.

  • Mutual funds and ETFs.
  • AIFs.
  • PMS versus mutual funds.
Unit 7 Role of portfolio managers10%

What a portfolio manager does and the three types of service.

  • Discretionary, non-discretionary and advisory.
  • Investment approach.
  • Role of distributors.
Unit 8 Operational aspects of portfolio managers13%

How a PMS actually operates: agreements, onboarding, fees, custody and reporting.

  • Portfolio management agreement.
  • Fees and exit load.
  • Custodian, reporting and disclosure.
Unit 9 Portfolio management process15%

The core method: objectives, asset allocation, security selection, implementation and review. The heaviest unit.

  • Steps of the process.
  • Asset allocation and rebalancing.
  • Risk management.
  • Behavioural traps.
Unit 10 Performance measurement and evaluation of portfolio managers10%

How to judge a PMS: returns, benchmarks, risk-adjusted measures and standard reporting.

  • TWRR and annualised return.
  • Benchmark and alpha.
  • Sharpe, drawdown, capture ratios.
Unit 11 Taxation5%

How PMS income is taxed in the client's hands.

  • Capital gains and business income.
  • Dividends and TDS.
  • Reporting.
Unit 12 Regulatory, governance and ethical aspects of portfolio managers10%

SEBI's Portfolio Managers Regulations, the conduct of managers and the distributor's duties.

  • Registration and net worth.
  • Code of conduct.
  • Distributor obligations.
  • Grievance redressal.

Q1. Which statement best describes “Investor charter”?

  1. Portfolio managers must provide an investor charter explaining the rights and obligations of clients and the grievance process.
  2. Large caps are generally more stable and liquid; small caps offer higher growth potential with more volatility and lower liquidity.
  3. Maximum drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value, showing worst-case loss experienced.
  4. Value investing buys stocks trading below estimated intrinsic value, expecting the price to converge.
