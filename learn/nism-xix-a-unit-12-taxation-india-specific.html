Key terms, examples and exam traps

Pass-through status

Category I and II AIFs enjoy pass-through status: income other than business income is taxed in the investors' hands, retaining its character.

Example. Capital gains of the fund are taxed in the investor's hands.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Category II AIFs pay tax on all income at the fund level.

Business income of AIF

Business income earned by a Category I or II AIF is taxed at the fund level and is not passed through.

Example. A fund's trading business profit is taxed at the fund.

Exam trap. This statement is false: All fund income is taxed in the investor's hands.

Category III taxation

Category III AIFs do not get pass-through status in the same way; income is generally taxed at the fund level as per the Act.

Example. A hedge fund's profits are taxed within the fund.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Category III AIFs are fully pass-through.

Character retained

Because of pass-through, gains retain their nature, such as long-term capital gains, when taxed in the investor's hands.

Example. A long-term gain in the fund remains long-term for the investor.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Gains lose their character after passing through.

TDS on distributions

Tax is deducted at source on income distributed to investors by pass-through AIFs at the rates prescribed, with credit available to the investor.

Example. The fund deducts tax at the time of distributing income.

Exam trap. This statement is false: AIFs never deduct TDS.

GST on fees

Management fees and certain charges to the fund may attract goods and services tax, affecting net returns.

Example. A fee invoice carries GST.

Exam trap. This statement is false: GST is not applicable on any fee.

Tax on carry

The manager's carried interest is taxed in its hands according to the nature of income, such as capital gains or business income.

Example. A manager's carry is reported in its own tax return.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Carry is taxed in the investors' hands.

Tax planning limits

Distributors should not provide tax advice beyond their remit; investors should consult a tax professional.

Example. A distributor suggests the client check with her tax adviser.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Distributors should give detailed tax opinions.

  1. Because of pass-through, gains retain their nature, such as long-term capital gains, when taxed in the investor's hands.
  2. An AIF scheme must have a corpus of at least ₹20 crore (₹5 crore for an angel fund) before it begins investing.
  3. Venture capital funds early-stage, high-growth companies, accepting high failure rates for the chance of outsized returns.

  1. Tax is deducted at source on income distributed to investors by pass-through AIFs at the rates prescribed, with credit available to the investor.

  3. Returns from alternatives depend heavily on manager skill, so dispersion between top and bottom managers is wide.
  4. An angel fund is a sub-category of Category I that pools money from angel investors to invest in early-stage start-ups, with smaller minimums.
