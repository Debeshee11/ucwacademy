Key terms, examples and exam traps

Securities

Securities include shares, bonds, units of mutual funds and derivatives, as defined in the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act.

Example. Shares and bonds are both examples.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Securities include only shares.

Primary and secondary market

New issues take place in the primary market; trading of already issued securities occurs in the secondary market.

Example. An IPO is a primary market event while daily trading is secondary.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The company receives money in secondary trades.

Stock exchange

A stock exchange is a recognised, regulated marketplace such as the BSE or NSE where securities trade electronically.

Example. Buyers and sellers are matched on an online system.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An exchange decides the profits of companies.

Trading member

A trading member is a broker admitted by the exchange who can trade on the platform for clients and own account.

Example. A SEBI-registered broker with exchange membership.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Any investor can be a trading member.

Depository

Depositories like NSDL and CDSL hold securities in electronic form through participants.

Example. A client's shares are held through a depository participant.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A depository is a type of stock exchange.

Clearing corporation

The clearing corporation guarantees and settles trades, becoming the central counterparty.

Example. It ensures delivery and payment for exchange trades.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The clearing corporation executes client orders.

Market segments

Exchanges offer segments for cash equity, equity derivatives, currency, commodity and debt trading.

Example. A client may trade shares in cash and futures in derivatives.

Exam trap. This statement is false: There is only a cash segment.

Retail and institutional participants

Participants include retail investors, high-net-worth individuals, mutual funds, FPIs, banks and insurers.

Example. Foreign portfolio investors and domestic mutual funds both trade.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Only retail investors trade on exchanges.

Dematerialisation

Dematerialisation converts paper certificates into electronic form, making trading and transfer faster and safer.

Example. A client's shares exist in electronic form.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Dematerialisation is the process of printing shares.
