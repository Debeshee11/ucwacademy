Key terms, examples and exam traps

Protective put

A protective put is buying a put against shares held, which limits the downside while keeping the upside.

Example. An investor holds shares and buys a put at a lower strike.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A protective put involves selling a put.

Covered call

A covered call is holding shares and selling a call, earning premium but capping the upside.

Example. A fund sells a call on shares it holds.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A covered call has no limit to the loss.

Long straddle

A long straddle buys a call and a put at the same strike and expiry, gaining from a big move in either direction.

Example. A trader expects a large move after results.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A long straddle gains when the price stays still.

Bull call spread

A bull call spread buys a lower strike call and sells a higher strike call, giving limited profit and limited loss.

Example. A trader mildly expects a rise.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A bull call spread has unlimited profit.

Bear put spread

A bear put spread buys a higher strike put and sells a lower strike put to profit from a moderate fall.

Example. A trader mildly expects a fall.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A bear put spread profits from a rise.

Long-short use

A long-short equity fund buys stocks expected to rise and uses futures or options to short those expected to fall, reducing market exposure.

Example. A SIF is long on strong stocks and short on index futures.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A long-short strategy is always fully long.

Short futures to hedge

Selling futures against a stock portfolio offsets losses on the portfolio if the market falls, though it gives up gains on a rise.

Example. A fund sells futures before an uncertain event.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Short futures add to losses when the market falls.

Arbitrage use

Equity arbitrage funds exploit price differences between cash and futures markets, with the futures sold against the shares bought.

Example. A fund buys shares and sells futures to lock the spread.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Arbitrage funds take full directional equity risk.

Derivative risk

Leverage in derivatives magnifies both gains and losses, so position limits and margins matter.

Example. A small market move causes a large margin loss.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Derivatives lower risk in every use.
