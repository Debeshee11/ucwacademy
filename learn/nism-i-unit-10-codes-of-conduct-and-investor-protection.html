Key terms, examples and exam traps

Code of conduct

Intermediaries must act with integrity, diligence and fairness and avoid manipulative practices.

Example. A broker refuses to execute a manipulative trade.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The code allows guaranteeing returns.

Risk disclosure

Brokers must inform clients of the risks, including leverage and potential losses beyond margin.

Example. A client signs the risk disclosure form.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Disclosure is needed only for large clients.

Suitability and mis-selling

Products should match the client's needs and understanding, and mis-selling is a violation.

Example. A dealer explains the risk of a short option.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A dealer may push complex products to all clients.

Front running

Trading ahead of a client's order for personal gain is prohibited.

Example. A dealer trades before executing a client order.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Front running is acceptable on small orders.

Investor Protection Fund

The exchange maintains a fund to compensate investors in case of a broker default up to specified limits.

Example. A client of a defaulting broker may claim compensation.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The fund covers market losses.

Confidentiality

Client data must be kept confidential and disclosed only as required by law.

Example. A dealer does not share client positions.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Client positions may be shared with other clients.

Complaint resolution

Complaints should be acknowledged and resolved within prescribed timelines.

Example. A firm responds to a complaint in time.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Complaints may be ignored.
