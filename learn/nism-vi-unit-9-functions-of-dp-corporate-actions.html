Key terms, examples and exam traps

Corporate action

A corporate action is an event by an issuer, such as a dividend, bonus, split, rights issue or merger, that affects securities and holders.

Example. A company announces a bonus issue to its shareholders.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A corporate action is a trade between two investors.

Record date

The record date is the date on which a holder must be on the company's records to be entitled to the benefit.

Example. A holder on the record date receives the dividend.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The record date is the date the benefit is paid.

Cash benefits

Cash benefits such as dividends are paid directly to the BO's bank account, using details held by the depository or issuer.

Example. A dividend is credited to the shareholder's bank account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Dividends are credited as securities to the demat account.

Non-cash benefits

Non-cash benefits such as bonus shares and split shares are credited to the demat account of the BO, using fresh ISINs where needed.

Example. Bonus shares appear in the demat account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Bonus shares are paid by cheque.

Stock split

A stock split reduces the face value of a share and increases the number of shares held, without changing total value.

Example. A ₹10 share is split into two shares of ₹5 each.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A split reduces the number of shares held.

Rights issue

In a rights issue, existing shareholders get the right to subscribe to new shares in proportion to their holdings.

Example. A holder is offered one new share for every four held.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A rights issue is open only to new investors.

Buyback

In a buyback the company purchases its own shares from holders, and the shares tendered are debited from the BO's account.

Example. A holder tenders her shares in the company's offer.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A buyback adds new shares to holders' accounts.
