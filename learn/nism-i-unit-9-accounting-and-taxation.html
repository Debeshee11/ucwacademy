Key terms, examples and exam traps

Business income

Profits and losses from exchange traded currency derivatives are generally treated as non-speculative business income.

Example. A trader reports gains as business income.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Gains are treated as capital gains.

Non-speculative loss

Non-speculative business losses can be set off against other income except salary, and carried forward for eight years.

Example. A trader carries forward unabsorbed losses.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Business losses can never be carried forward.

Turnover for audit

Turnover from derivatives is computed on the basis of the absolute sum of profits and losses, with audit rules based on thresholds.

Example. A trader calculates turnover for tax audit.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Turnover is the total contract value.

Accounting at settlement

Daily mark-to-market gains and losses are recognised in accounts on settlement or at the balance sheet date for open positions.

Example. Unsettled positions are marked at year end.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Accounting ignores open positions.

Transaction costs

Brokerage, exchange charges, GST and stamp duty apply to currency derivative trades.

Example. The contract note shows these charges.

Exam trap. This statement is false: There are no costs besides brokerage.

Record maintenance

Traders should maintain contract notes, margin statements and ledgers to substantiate returns.

Example. A trader keeps statements for tax filing.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Records are not needed for tax filing.
