Key terms, examples and exam traps

Sensex

The Sensex is the BSE's 30-stock benchmark, weighted by free-float market capitalisation.

Example. An index of 30 companies on the Bombay exchange.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The Sensex has 50 stocks.

Nifty 50

The Nifty 50 represents 50 large companies on the NSE, weighted by free-float market capitalisation.

Example. An index of 50 leading companies on the National exchange.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The Nifty 50 has 30 stocks.

Free-float method

The free-float method counts only publicly available shares, excluding promoter holdings, to weight stocks in an index.

Example. A stock with high promoter holding gets less weight.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The free-float method counts all shares.

Sectoral and broad indices

Sectoral indices track specific industries like banks, while broad indices such as the Nifty 500 represent the wider market.

Example. An index tracking only banks is sectoral.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Sectoral indices cover the whole market.

Market breadth

Breadth compares the number of advancing and declining stocks, showing how widespread a market move is.

Example. The index rises but declining stocks outnumber advancing ones.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Breadth shows the daily turnover.

India VIX

India VIX measures the market's expectation of near-term volatility, derived from index option prices.

Example. A rising reading indicates greater expected volatility.

Exam trap. This statement is false: India VIX measures interest rates.

Market turnover

Turnover is the total value of securities traded in a period, showing market activity and liquidity.

Example. The day's total traded value in the cash market.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Turnover is the total market capitalisation.

FPI and DII flows

Net buying or selling by FPIs and DIIs is tracked daily as it influences market direction.

Example. Foreign investors sell heavily and the index falls.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Institutional flows have no effect on prices.

Market P/E

The index P/E divides the index's total market value by total earnings and shows whether the market is richly valued.

Example. The Nifty trades at 22 times earnings.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Index P/E shows the number of stocks.

52-week high and low

The 52-week high and low are the highest and lowest prices in the past year, used as reference for momentum and risk.

Example. A stock hits a new yearly high.

Exam trap. This statement is false: 52-week high is the highest price ever.

Put-call ratio

The put-call ratio compares put and call volumes or open interest and is a sentiment indicator.

Example. A high ratio suggests defensive positioning.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The put-call ratio measures dividends.

Impact cost

Impact cost is the cost of executing a standard-size order beyond the ideal price and measures liquidity.

Example. A large order moves the price against the buyer.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Impact cost is the brokerage charged.

Beta of stock

Beta shows how sensitive a stock is to market movements; above 1 is more volatile than the index.

Example. A stock moves 1.5 times the market.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A beta of 1 means the stock is risk free.
