Key terms, examples and exam traps

Taxation at client level

PMS transactions are in the client's own name, so gains and losses are taxed directly in the client's hands.

Example. Each sale by the manager creates a tax event for the client.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The PMS entity pays tax on behalf of all clients.

Short-term and long-term gains

Gains on listed shares held up to 12 months are short-term and over 12 months long-term, with different tax rates.

Example. A share sold after 14 months gives a long-term gain.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The holding period for listed shares is 36 months.

Dividend income

Dividend received on shares in the portfolio is taxable in the client's hands at the slab rate.

Example. A client adds dividend credited to her account to income.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Dividends are tax-free for all clients.

Capital gains statement

Managers provide a statement of realised gains and losses, which helps the client file her tax return.

Example. A client uses the statement to compute her gains.

Exam trap. This statement is false: No tax statement is provided.

Set-off of losses

Capital losses can be set off against capital gains and carried forward as per the tax law.

Example. A loss on one sale offsets a gain on another.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Losses can never be set off.

Tax on fees

Management fees and expenses may be deductible against gains or income as per the law, subject to conditions.

Example. A client considers the tax treatment of fees paid.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Fees are always deductible.

Turnover and tax

High portfolio turnover can push more gains into short-term, increasing the tax cost.

Example. A manager who trades frequently generates more short-term gains.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Turnover has no tax effect.
