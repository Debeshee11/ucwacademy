Key terms, examples and exam traps

RBI role

The RBI regulates foreign exchange and the currency derivatives framework along with SEBI.

Example. The RBI issues rules on forex and eligible participants.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The RBI has no role in currency derivatives.

SEBI role

SEBI regulates exchange-traded currency derivatives, including exchanges, brokers and trading rules.

Example. SEBI approves contract specifications.

Exam trap. This statement is false: SEBI regulates only mutual funds.

FEMA

The Foreign Exchange Management Act governs foreign exchange transactions in India.

Example. Cross-border payments follow FEMA rules.

Exam trap. This statement is false: FEMA is a tax law.

Underlying exposure

Residents may hold positions in some pairs freely, while in others positions beyond limits require proof of underlying exposure.

Example. A trader with no exposure stays within the cap.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Exposure proof is never required.

Eligible pairs

Exchange traded contracts are available only in pairs permitted by the RBI and SEBI, including INR pairs and certain cross-currency pairs.

Example. USD-INR and EUR-INR contracts are listed.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Any currency pair can be listed.

Position limits for clients

Limits for individual clients are set at a set dollar amount of gross open position per currency pair.

Example. Retail position caps apply.

Exam trap. This statement is false: There are no client-level limits.

Broker obligations

Brokers must follow KYC, risk disclosure and record-keeping rules under SEBI and exchange regulations.

Example. A broker gives the client a risk disclosure document.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Brokers may skip KYC for small clients.

Grievance redressal

Investors can approach the broker, then the exchange and SEBI to resolve complaints.

Example. A client escalates an unresolved complaint to the exchange.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Clients cannot complain about brokers.
