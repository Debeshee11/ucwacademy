Understanding securities markets and performance: NISM Series XII notes and practice questions

Securities markets move savings of households into the investments of companies and governments. This chapter explains why markets exist, who takes part, and who keeps them honest.

Key terms, examples and exam traps

Securities market

The securities market is where securities such as shares, bonds and units are issued and traded, channelling savings into productive investment.

Example. A company raises money by selling shares to the public, and investors later trade those shares.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The securities market deals only in physical goods.

Primary market

The primary market is where new securities are issued for the first time and the issuer receives the money.

Example. A company launches an IPO and receives funds from investors.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Investors trade with each other in the primary market.

Secondary market

The secondary market is where existing securities are bought and sold among investors, usually on a stock exchange, providing liquidity.

Example. An investor sells shares she bought last year to another investor on an exchange.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The issuing company receives the money when shares trade in the secondary market.

Money market

The money market deals in short-term debt instruments with a maturity of up to one year, such as treasury bills and commercial paper.

Example. A company raises funds for 90 days by issuing a short-term instrument.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The money market deals in securities of 10 years or more.

Capital market

The capital market deals in long-term securities such as equity shares and bonds with maturities beyond one year.

Example. A company issues 10-year bonds to fund a new factory.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The capital market deals only in securities of less than one year.

SEBI

The Securities and Exchange Board of India regulates the securities market, protects investors and promotes market development.

Example. A broker that violates trading rules is penalised by the market regulator.

Exam trap. This statement is false: SEBI sets the repo rate.

Market participants

Participants include issuers, investors, brokers, depositories, clearing corporations, merchant bankers, mutual funds and regulators.

Example. An investor buys through a broker, with units held at a depository and trades settled by a clearing corporation.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The only participants in the securities market are investors and issuers.

Financial intermediation

Financial intermediaries connect savers and borrowers and add value through pooling, risk management and information.

Example. A bank takes deposits from savers and lends to borrowers.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Intermediaries add no value to the financial system.

Market efficiency

An efficient market reflects available information in prices quickly, making it hard to consistently earn abnormal returns.

Example. Share prices adjust within seconds of a company announcing results.

Exam trap. This statement is false: In an efficient market prices never move.

Market performance indicators

Indices, market capitalisation, turnover and the number of investors are used to assess how a market is performing.

Example. An analyst reviews the Sensex and daily turnover to judge market activity.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Market performance is measured only by the price of one share.
