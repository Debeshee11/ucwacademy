Key terms, examples and exam traps

Trading hours

Currency derivatives trade on weekdays from 9:00 am to 5:00 pm, subject to exchange schedule.

Example. A trader places an order at 4 pm.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Currency derivatives trade round the clock.

Order types

Exchanges allow market, limit and stop-loss orders, with validity such as day or immediate or cancel.

Example. A trader places a limit order at a stated rate.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Only market orders are allowed.

Order matching

Orders are matched on price-time priority in an electronic order book.

Example. The best priced and earliest order is filled first.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Orders are matched manually by the exchange.

Trading members and clients

Trading members execute for clients, and clients must be registered with unique client codes mapped to PAN.

Example. A client trades through a registered broker.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Clients may trade directly without any member.

Permitted participants

Resident individuals and entities may trade in exchange traded currency derivatives only with underlying exposure limits for certain pairs, and FPIs have separate limits.

Example. A trader without exposure is limited to a position cap.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Anyone may take unlimited positions.

Operating range

Exchanges set daily price bands to restrict extreme movements in contract prices.

Example. An order outside the range is rejected.

Exam trap. This statement is false: There is no limit on daily price movement.

Price bands and circuit breakers

If prices move beyond set limits, trading can be paused to calm the market.

Example. Trading halts after a sharp fall.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Circuit breakers apply only to equities.

Trade confirmation

Trades are confirmed through contract notes issued by the broker.

Example. A client receives a contract note after execution.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Contract notes are issued monthly.

Client code modification

Modifying client codes after trade is restricted and subject to penalties, to prevent misuse.

Example. A broker cannot change codes freely to shift trades.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Client codes can be changed any time.

Contracts on offer

Different currency pairs and expiry months are available, with near-month contracts typically being most liquid.

Example. Near-month USD-INR futures see heavy trading.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Far-month contracts are the most liquid.
