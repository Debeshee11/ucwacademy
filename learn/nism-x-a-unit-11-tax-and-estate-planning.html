Key terms, examples and exam traps

Residential status

Tax liability in India depends on residential status — resident, non-resident or not ordinarily resident — which decides what income is taxable here.

Example. A person who spends more than 182 days in India in a year is generally a resident.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Residential status depends only on citizenship.

Heads of income

Income is classified under five heads: salary, house property, business or profession, capital gains and other sources.

Example. Rent from a flat and interest on a deposit fall under different heads.

Exam trap. This statement is false: All income is taxed under one head.

Capital gains

Capital gains are profits on the sale of capital assets, classified as short-term or long-term based on the holding period of the asset.

Example. An investor sells shares for more than she paid.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Capital gains arise only from selling property.

Section 80C

Section 80C allows deductions up to a ceiling for specified investments and expenses under the old tax regime, such as PPF, ELSS and life insurance premium.

Example. An investor claims a deduction for ELSS investment.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Section 80C is available under both regimes always.

New versus old regime

Taxpayers can choose between the default new regime with lower slab rates and few deductions, and the old regime with higher rates and many deductions.

Example. A client compares tax payable under both regimes before choosing.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The old regime has no deductions.

Tax-loss harvesting

Tax-loss harvesting means selling investments at a loss to set off against gains, reducing the tax payable.

Example. A client books a loss in one stock to offset a gain in another.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Tax-loss harvesting increases tax payable.

Set-off and carry forward

Capital losses can be set off against capital gains and carried forward for eight years, subject to conditions.

Example. A loss this year is used to reduce gains next year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Losses can never be carried forward.

TDS

Tax deducted at source is tax withheld by the payer on specified payments such as salary and interest, and credited to the payee's tax account.

Example. A bank deducts tax on fixed deposit interest above the limit.

Exam trap. This statement is false: TDS is paid by the receiver in cash at year end.

Advance tax

Advance tax is paid in instalments during the year by taxpayers whose liability exceeds a threshold.

Example. A trader pays tax quarterly instead of at year end.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Advance tax is paid only after filing the return.

Will

A will is a legal document by which a person decides how her property is to be distributed after death, and it takes effect only on death.

Example. An investor states which heirs get which assets.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A will takes effect immediately on signing.

Intestate succession

When a person dies without a will, the property passes according to the succession laws applicable to the person's religion.

Example. A man dies with no will and his assets pass as per the law.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Without a will, the government takes all property.

Probate

Probate is the court's certificate that proves a will is genuine and authorises the executor to administer the estate.

Example. An executor obtains court proof before dealing with the assets.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Probate is the process of writing a will.

Nominee versus legal heir

A nominee is only a custodian who receives the asset on death and must hand it to the legal heirs; she is not necessarily the owner.

Example. A nominee receives proceeds but must pass them on as per the will.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A nominee becomes the sole owner of the asset.

Trust

A trust holds assets for beneficiaries under a trustee's control, and is used for succession, protection and management of wealth.

Example. A parent places assets under a trustee to benefit a child later.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A trust has no beneficiaries.

HUF

A Hindu Undivided Family is a separate taxable entity consisting of family members from a common ancestor, which can hold assets and file its own return.

Example. A family unit with its own PAN and tax return.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An HUF is a company.

Gift tax rules

Gifts received from specified relatives are exempt from tax, while gifts above a threshold from others can be taxed as income of the receiver.

Example. A son receives money from his father with no tax.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Gifts from relatives are taxed at 30%.

Power of attorney

A power of attorney authorises another person to act for the principal in specified matters, and ends on the principal's death.

Example. An elderly client authorises her son to handle bank formalities.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A power of attorney continues after the principal's death.

Clubbing of income

Income earned from assets gifted to a spouse or minor child is generally clubbed with the giver's income for tax.

Example. A husband gifts money to his wife who invests it, and the income is added to his.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Income of a minor child is always taxed separately.
