  • PMLA and KYC.

Key terms, examples and exam traps

SEBI

SEBI protects investors, promotes market development and regulates the securities market, with powers to make regulations, investigate and penalise.

Example. The regulator can issue circulars and levy penalties on brokers.

Exam trap. This statement is false: SEBI is a stock exchange.

SCRA 1956

The Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act governs recognition of stock exchanges and regulates the trading of securities.

Example. The law under which exchanges are recognised.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The SCRA is the law on money laundering.

Depositories Act 1996

This law provides the legal framework for depositories and dematerialisation of securities in India.

Example. The law behind NSDL and CDSL.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The Depositories Act governs insurance companies.

Stock broker regulations

Brokers must be registered with SEBI, be members of an exchange, meet capital adequacy and follow a code of conduct.

Example. A broker without registration cannot deal for clients.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A broker needs no registration if it is an exchange member.

Client registration form

Each client must sign the account opening form with mandatory documents and KYC, and the broker must give a copy of the rights and obligations.

Example. A client signs the standard form before the first trade.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Clients may trade before signing the form.

Know Your Client

KYC verifies identity and address using PAN and documents, and is mandatory for opening any trading account.

Example. A new client provides PAN, address proof and a photograph.

Exam trap. This statement is false: KYC is optional for small investors.

Unique Client Code

The UCC is a unique code allotted to each client by the broker and mapped to the client's PAN in exchange records.

Example. Each client's trades are tagged with a code mapped to PAN.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Clients share one code with the broker.

Contract note

A broker must issue a contract note for every trade within 24 hours of execution, showing price, time, quantity and charges.

Example. A client gets a note after executing a trade.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A contract note is issued at the end of the year.

Power of attorney

A POA authorises the broker to perform limited acts such as pledging for margin; it must not give general trading authority and misuse is prohibited.

Example. A client signs a limited authority for settlement-related acts.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A POA allows brokers to trade at will for the client.

Running account settlement

Brokers must settle client funds and securities at regular intervals, monthly or quarterly as chosen by the client, unless the client specifies otherwise.

Example. A broker returns the unused client balance on schedule.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Brokers may hold client balances indefinitely.

Segregation of client funds

Client money and securities must be kept separate from the broker's own and cannot be used for proprietary trading.

Example. A broker keeps clients' money in a separate account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A broker may use client funds for own trades.

Insider trading regulations

SEBI's PIT regulations prohibit dealing in securities while in possession of unpublished price sensitive information.

Example. A broker's employee trades on a leaked merger news.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Insiders may trade if they trade small amounts.

Fraudulent and unfair trade practices

The PFUTP regulations prohibit market manipulation, misleading statements and fraud in securities dealing.

Example. Spreading false rumours to move a stock price.

Exam trap. This statement is false: PFUTP regulations apply only to issuers.

PMLA

The Prevention of Money Laundering Act requires brokers to verify customers, maintain records and report suspicious transactions to the FIU.

Example. A broker reports an unusual cash-related pattern to the authority.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Brokers need not report suspicious transactions.

Suspicious transaction report

A broker must report suspicious transactions to the Financial Intelligence Unit when it suspects money laundering.

Example. A broker files a report on a client with unexplained large flows.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The client must be told before a report is filed.

Investor charter

Brokers must display and share the investor charter listing investors' rights, responsibilities and grievance steps.

Example. A client receives a document describing her rights.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The investor charter is for brokers' internal use.

Arbitration

Disputes between clients and brokers can be referred to the exchange's arbitration mechanism within the prescribed period.

Example. A client takes an unresolved dispute to arbitration.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Clients cannot resolve disputes through exchanges.

SCORES and ODR

Clients can lodge complaints with SEBI through SCORES and use the online dispute resolution platform after approaching the broker and the exchange.

Example. A client files a complaint on the regulator's portal.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Complaints can only be made in person.

Investor Protection Fund

Exchanges maintain a fund to compensate clients of defaulting brokers up to a limit.

Example. A client of a defaulting broker may claim compensation from the fund.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The fund covers all trading losses.

Code of conduct

The code of conduct for brokers requires integrity, diligence, fair dealing and proper records, and prohibits unfair practices.

Example. A broker must not promise guaranteed profits.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The code allows brokers to guarantee returns.

Margin trading facility

Brokers may offer margin funding under SEBI rules with disclosures, collecting margin and keeping the pledged securities in a separate account.

Example. A client buys shares by funding part with the broker's money.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Margin funding needs no margin from the client.

Authorised person

Authorised persons act on behalf of brokers for acquiring clients and facilitating trades, as registered with the exchange.

Example. An agent introduces clients to the broker.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Authorised persons may execute trades on their own.

Record retention

Brokers must maintain records of orders, trades, communications and client details for at least five years.

Example. A broker keeps the call recording of a client order.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Records can be deleted after a month.
