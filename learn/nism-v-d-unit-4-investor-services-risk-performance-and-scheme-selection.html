Key terms, examples and exam traps

KYC

Investors must complete KYC with proof of identity and address and PAN before investing in funds.

Example. A first-time investor completes KYC before investing.

Exam trap. This statement is false: KYC is only needed above ₹50,000 and only for equity funds.

Systematic investment plan

A SIP invests a fixed amount at regular intervals, averaging cost across market ups and downs.

Example. An investor puts ₹5,000 every month.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A SIP guarantees profits.

Systematic withdrawal plan

A SWP lets an investor take a fixed amount at regular intervals by redeeming units.

Example. A retiree withdraws ₹20,000 every month.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A SWP puts money into the scheme monthly.

Systematic transfer plan

A STP moves a fixed amount at regular intervals from one scheme to another.

Example. An investor moves ₹10,000 monthly from a liquid fund to an equity fund.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A STP withdraws money to the bank.

Standard deviation

Standard deviation measures how much returns vary around their average, so a higher value means a more volatile fund.

Example. A fund with 18% standard deviation swings more than one with 8%.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A higher standard deviation means safer returns.

Beta

Beta measures a fund's sensitivity to the market, where 1.2 means a move 20% larger than the market.

Example. A fund with beta 1.2 rises 12% when the market rises 10%.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Beta is the fund's absolute return.

Sharpe ratio

The Sharpe ratio measures excess return over the risk-free rate per unit of total risk.

Example. A higher Sharpe ratio means better risk-adjusted performance.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A lower Sharpe ratio means better risk-adjusted performance.

CAGR

CAGR is the annualised rate at which an investment grows over a period longer than a year.

Example. ₹100 growing to ₹121 in two years is a 10% CAGR.

Exam trap. This statement is false: CAGR is the simple total gain divided by years.

Benchmark and tracking

A fund's performance should be compared with its stated benchmark over comparable periods and consistently, not for one period only.

Example. An adviser compares a fund with its benchmark over five years.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A fund can be judged by its last month's return.

Risk profiling

Risk profiling assesses a client's capacity and willingness to take risk to guide product choice.

Example. A distributor asks how the client would feel if the portfolio fell 25%.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Every client of the same age has the same risk profile.

Suitability

A distributor must recommend products that suit the client's goals, horizon, risk profile and financial situation.

Example. A conservative client is not pushed into a high-risk fund.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The highest commission product is the suitable one.
