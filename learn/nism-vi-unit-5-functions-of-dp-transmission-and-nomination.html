Key terms, examples and exam traps

Nomination

Nomination lets an account holder name a person who will receive the securities if the holder dies.

Example. A holder names her son as nominee when opening the account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A nominee becomes owner of the securities while the holder is alive.

Number of nominees

SEBI allows an investor to nominate up to ten persons for a demat account, with the percentage share of each specified.

Example. An investor splits holdings among three nominees in stated proportions.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Only one nominee is permitted for a demat account.

Transmission

Transmission is the transfer of securities to a person by operation of law, such as on the death of a holder, and not by sale or gift.

Example. Securities of a deceased holder pass to the nominee or legal heir.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Transmission is a transfer for consideration.

Joint holders

On the death of one joint holder, the surviving joint holder is recognised as the holder of the securities.

Example. The second holder continues to hold the securities after the first holder's death.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The securities of a joint account are frozen permanently on a holder's death.

Documents for transmission

A transmission request needs a claim form, an attested death certificate and identity and KYC details of the claimant, with extra documents when there is no nominee.

Example. The nominee submits the death certificate with her claim.

Exam trap. This statement is false: No documents are required for transmission.

Nominee and legal heir

A nominee receives the securities as a trustee for the legal heirs when the law provides so, while a legal heir has a right under succession law.

Example. A nominee must account to the legal heirs where succession rules say so.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A nominee always owns the securities outright against other legal heirs.

Nomination for minors

A nominee may be a minor, with a guardian appointed to act for her.

Example. A holder nominates her grandchild and names the child's guardian.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A minor cannot be a nominee under any circumstance.
