Key terms, examples and exam traps

Market manipulation

Market manipulation is any activity that artificially moves prices or volumes, such as rigging or spreading false news.

Example. A group trades among themselves to inflate a stock's volume.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Market manipulation is legal if the trades are small.

Pump and dump

In a pump and dump, promoters inflate a stock's price with hype, then sell to the public at the high.

Example. A stock is promoted by misleading messages before insiders sell.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A pump and dump is a legitimate strategy.

Front running

Front running is trading ahead of a client's large order to profit from the expected price impact.

Example. A dealer buys for himself before executing a client's big buy order.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Front running is permitted if the client is not harmed.

Surveillance alerts

Exchanges and brokers generate alerts on unusual price, volume, client concentration or order patterns.

Example. A system flags a sudden surge in a stock's volume.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Surveillance covers only derivatives.

Graded surveillance measure

GSM places stocks with a mismatch between price and fundamentals in graded stages with restrictions on trading.

Example. A stock whose price is far above earnings is placed in a restricted stage.

Exam trap. This statement is false: GSM is a reward for strong stocks.

Reporting obligations

Brokers must report suspicious trades and clients to the exchange and regulators and keep records of alerts and actions.

Example. A broker flags a series of suspicious trades to the exchange.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Brokers need not report anything about client trades.

Penalties for abuse

SEBI can impose monetary penalties, bar participants from the market and prosecute those involved in abuse.

Example. A manipulator is barred and fined.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Market abuse carries no penalty.

Know your client and surveillance

KYC details help the broker spot clients whose trading pattern does not match their declared profile.

Example. A student with a small income suddenly trades large amounts.

Exam trap. This statement is false: KYC has no role in surveillance.
