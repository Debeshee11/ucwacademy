Key terms, examples and exam traps

Passive strategy

A passive strategy replicates an index to match its return at low cost without trying to outperform.

Example. A portfolio holds the index stocks in index weights.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A passive strategy seeks to beat the index.

Active strategy

An active strategy departs from the benchmark to earn higher returns, at higher cost and risk of underperformance.

Example. A manager overweights chosen stocks.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Active strategies guarantee outperformance.

Smart beta

Smart beta uses rules-based weights based on factors such as value or low volatility instead of market capitalisation.

Example. An index weights stocks by earnings rather than size.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Smart beta is a market-cap weighted index.

Factor investing

Factor investing targets return drivers such as value, momentum, quality and size across stocks.

Example. A portfolio tilts to high-quality stocks.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Factor investing ignores stock characteristics.

Core-satellite

A core-satellite approach holds a low-cost core, often passive, with smaller active satellites to seek extra return.

Example. A manager holds an index core and a few active bets.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Core-satellite is 100% active.

Growth and value styles

Growth investing seeks firms with high earnings growth, while value investing seeks stocks trading below their estimated worth.

Example. A manager buys a low P/E stock with solid earnings.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Value investing seeks the highest P/E.

Bond immunisation

Immunisation matches the portfolio's duration to the liability horizon so that interest rate changes have little net effect.

Example. A manager matches duration to a payout date.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Immunisation aims to maximise rate bets.

Laddering

A bond ladder spreads holdings evenly across maturities to manage reinvestment and interest rate risk.

Example. Bonds mature every year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A ladder puts all funds in one maturity.

Barbell and bullet

A barbell holds short and long maturities, while a bullet concentrates holdings around one maturity.

Example. A barbell skips the intermediate bonds.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A bullet spreads across all maturities.

Active bond management

Active bond managers use views on rates, curve shape and credit spreads to earn more than the benchmark.

Example. A manager lengthens duration before rates fall.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Active bond managers never change duration.

Derivative overlay

Derivatives can be overlaid on a portfolio to adjust exposure or protect it without selling the underlying securities.

Example. A manager sells index futures to cut equity exposure.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Overlays require selling all holdings.
