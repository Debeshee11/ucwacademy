Key terms, examples and exam traps

Risk and return

Higher expected return generally needs higher risk, and investors are compensated for bearing risk that cannot be diversified away.

Example. A client who wants high returns is told to expect bigger falls too.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A high return can be earned with no added risk.

Inflation and real return

Real return is the return after adjusting for inflation, so it shows the rise in purchasing power.

Example. An 8% return with 6% inflation gives roughly 2% real return.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Nominal return already shows purchasing power.

Mutual fund

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it in securities according to a stated objective, managed by professionals.

Example. Thousands of investors share one diversified portfolio.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A mutual fund guarantees a fixed return to investors.

Unit holders and units

Investors own units of a scheme in proportion to their money invested, and share its gains, losses and expenses.

Example. An investor holding 1% of a scheme's units owns 1% of its portfolio.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Unit holders own a specific share held by the fund.

Three-tier structure

A mutual fund has a sponsor, a trust with trustees and an asset management company, with a custodian holding the securities.

Example. The trustees oversee the AMC which manages the schemes.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The AMC owns the fund assets in its own name.

Role of trustees

Trustees are responsible for protecting unit holders' interests and for overseeing the AMC's compliance.

Example. Trustees review the AMC's reports every quarter.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Trustees pick the stocks for each scheme.

Role of custodian

A custodian holds the scheme's securities in safe custody and settles transactions, separate from the AMC.

Example. Securities of a scheme are kept with an independent custodian.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The AMC holds securities in its own accounts.

Advantages of mutual funds

Funds give diversification, professional management, liquidity, affordability and transparency in one product.

Example. An investor with small savings holds dozens of stocks through one fund.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Funds remove all investment risk.

Open-ended and closed-ended

Open-ended schemes accept entries and exits at NAV on any business day, while closed-ended schemes have a fixed maturity and are listed.

Example. An investor redeems an open-ended scheme at that day's NAV.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Closed-ended schemes can be redeemed from the AMC any day.
