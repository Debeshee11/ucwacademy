Key terms, examples and exam traps

Forward contract

A forward contract is a customised agreement to buy or sell a currency at a fixed rate on a future date, traded over the counter.

Example. An importer books dollars for delivery in three months.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A forward contract is traded on an exchange with standard terms.

Forward premium and discount

A currency trades at a forward premium if the forward rate is above spot and at a discount if below.

Example. With rupee interest rates above dollar rates, USD-INR forwards trade at a premium.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A forward premium means the forward is below spot.

Interest rate parity

Interest rate parity links the forward rate to spot and the interest rate differential, so the currency with higher interest rate trades at a forward discount.

Example. The forward rate reflects the difference in rupee and dollar interest rates.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Forward rates are independent of interest rates.

Forward rate formula

Forward rate is approximately spot x (1 + rupee rate x t) / (1 + dollar rate x t).

Example. Spot 83 with a higher rupee rate gives a forward above 83.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Forward rate equals spot always.

Currency swap

A currency swap exchanges principal and interest in one currency for another over a period.

Example. Two firms swap loans in different currencies.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A swap is an exchange-traded futures contract.

Non-deliverable forward

An NDF is settled in cash in a convertible currency based on the difference between the contract rate and a fixing rate, with no delivery of the restricted currency.

Example. Offshore parties trade rupee NDFs settled in dollars.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An NDF requires physical delivery.

OTC versus exchange traded

OTC contracts are customised and carry counterparty risk, while exchange-traded contracts are standardised and guaranteed by the clearing corporation.

Example. A futures trade is guaranteed by the clearing corporation.

Exam trap. This statement is false: OTC contracts are guaranteed by the exchange.

Hedging with forwards

An exporter expecting dollars sells them forward to fix the rupee amount, removing the risk of rupee appreciation.

Example. An exporter locks in a rate for payment due in six months.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Hedging removes all possible gains and costs.

Exposure types

Transaction, translation and economic exposure describe how currency changes affect cash flows, balance sheets and long-term value.

Example. A firm with dollar debt faces transaction exposure.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Exposure only arises for exporters.

Currency options OTC

Banks also offer OTC currency options that are customised for corporates.

Example. A corporate buys a tailored dollar call from a bank.

Exam trap. This statement is false: OTC options are always exchange guaranteed.

Q2. Which statement best describes “Interest rate parity”?

  1. Regimes range from fixed to floating; India follows a managed float where the central bank may intervene to curb volatility.
  2. The contract size of USD-INR futures is 1,000 US dollars per lot.

  4. Interest rate parity links the forward rate to spot and the interest rate differential, so the currency with higher interest rate trades at a forward discount.

Q3. Which statement best describes “Forward premium and discount”?

  1. If prices move beyond set limits, trading can be paused to calm the market.
  2. A currency appreciates when it buys more foreign currency and depreciates when it buys less.
  3. In a bull call spread, maximum profit equals the difference in strikes less the net premium.
  4. A currency trades at a forward premium if the forward rate is above spot and at a discount if below.

Q5. Which statement best describes “Currency swap”?

  1. Rolling over means closing a near-month position and opening a similar one in a later month to maintain exposure.
  2. Orders are matched on price-time priority in an electronic order book.
  3. A currency swap exchanges principal and interest in one currency for another over a period.
  4. Residents may hold positions in some pairs freely, while in others positions beyond limits require proof of underlying exposure.

  1. Orders are matched on price-time priority in an electronic order book.
  2. Forward rate is approximately spot x (1 + rupee rate x t) / (1 + dollar rate x t).
  3. Options let a hedger fix a worst-case rate while keeping the benefit of favourable moves, at the cost of the premium.
