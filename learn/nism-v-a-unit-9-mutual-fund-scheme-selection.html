Key terms, examples and exam traps

Large cap fund

A large cap fund invests at least 80% of its assets in the top 100 companies by market capitalisation.

Example. A scheme holds 85% in the 100 biggest listed companies.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A large cap fund invests mainly in companies ranked 251 onwards.

Mid cap fund

A mid cap fund invests at least 65% in companies ranked 101 to 250 by market capitalisation.

Example. A scheme keeps 70% in stocks ranked 101 to 250.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A mid cap fund invests mainly in the top 100 companies.

Small cap fund

A small cap fund invests at least 65% in companies ranked 251 and below by market capitalisation.

Example. A scheme invests mostly in companies outside the top 250.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A small cap fund must hold at least 80% in the top 100.

Multi cap fund

A multi cap fund must invest at least 25% each in large, mid and small cap stocks, and at least 75% in equity overall.

Example. A scheme must hold a quarter in each of three market-cap bands.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A multi cap fund can invest everything in large caps.

Flexi cap fund

A flexi cap fund invests at least 65% in equity with no restriction on market-cap allocation, so the manager can move across large, mid and small cap.

Example. A scheme can shift freely between large and small companies.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A flexi cap fund must hold 25% in each market-cap band.

Focused fund

A focused fund invests in a maximum of 30 stocks and at least 65% in equity, taking concentrated positions.

Example. A scheme holds just 25 stocks in its portfolio.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A focused fund holds more than 100 stocks.

ELSS

An Equity Linked Savings Scheme invests at least 80% in equity and has a three-year lock-in, and qualifies for the old tax regime deduction under Section 80C.

Example. An investor locks money in for three years to claim a deduction.

Exam trap. This statement is false: ELSS has a lock-in of one year.

Sectoral/thematic fund

A sectoral or thematic fund invests at least 80% in a particular sector or theme, such as banking or technology, and carries concentration risk.

Example. A scheme puts 85% of its money in a single industry.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A thematic fund is well diversified across all sectors.

Value and contra funds

A value fund follows a value strategy, and a contra fund follows a contrarian one, each investing at least 65% in equity; a fund house can offer only one of these two.

Example. A scheme buys out-of-favour stocks believing they are undervalued.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A value fund buys only stocks at record highs.

Overnight fund

An overnight fund invests in securities with a maturity of one day, so interest-rate and credit risk are very low.

Example. A scheme holds paper that matures the next day.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An overnight fund holds bonds of ten years' maturity.

Liquid fund

A liquid fund invests in debt and money-market securities with a maturity of up to 91 days.

Example. A scheme holds only paper maturing within three months.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A liquid fund holds securities of up to three years.

Gilt fund

A gilt fund invests at least 80% in government securities across maturities, so credit risk is negligible but interest-rate risk can be high.

Example. A scheme holds mostly central and state government bonds.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A gilt fund invests mainly in low-rated corporate bonds.

Credit risk fund

A credit risk fund invests at least 65% in corporate bonds rated AA and below, seeking higher yields at higher credit risk.

Example. A scheme chases higher yield by lending to lower-rated companies.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A credit risk fund holds only AAA-rated bonds.

Corporate bond fund

A corporate bond fund invests at least 80% in the highest-rated (AA+ and above) corporate bonds.

Example. A scheme holds mostly bonds of top-rated companies.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A corporate bond fund holds mostly AA-rated and below paper.

Aggressive hybrid fund

An aggressive hybrid fund invests 65% to 80% in equity and 20% to 35% in debt.

Example. A scheme keeps about 70% in equity and the rest in debt.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An aggressive hybrid fund holds less than 40% in equity.

Conservative hybrid fund

A conservative hybrid fund invests 10% to 25% in equity and 75% to 90% in debt.

Example. A scheme keeps 20% in equity and 80% in debt.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A conservative hybrid fund keeps 70% in equity.

Arbitrage fund

An arbitrage fund invests at least 65% in equity and equity-related instruments and profits from price differences between cash and futures markets; it is taxed as equity.

Example. A fund buys a share in the cash market and sells its future at a higher price.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An arbitrage fund takes a large one-sided view on stocks.

Index fund and ETF

An index fund or ETF replicates a market index, investing at least 95% in the index's securities, with low cost and no stock picking.

Example. A scheme mirrors the Nifty 50 with very low costs.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An index fund tries to beat its index through stock picking.

Solution-oriented funds

Retirement and children's funds have a lock-in of five years or until retirement age or the child turns 18, whichever is earlier.

Example. A scheme designed for a child's education has a five-year lock-in.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Solution-oriented funds have no lock-in.

Selection criteria

A good selection looks at risk-adjusted return, consistency over several periods, expense ratio, portfolio quality, fund manager continuity and fit with the investor's profile.

Example. A distributor compares funds on several measures, not just last year's return.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Last year's top performer is always the best choice.

Q1. Which term is described here? A mid cap fund invests at least 65% in companies ranked 101 to 250 by market capitalisation.

  1. Asset allocation pattern
  2. NPS
  3. Risk in mutual funds
  4. Mid cap fund

Q2. Which term is described here? An Equity Linked Savings Scheme invests at least 80% in equity and has a three-year lock-in, and qualifies for the old tax regime deduction under Section 80C.

  1. Disclosure of commission
  2. ELSS
  3. Rupee cost averaging
  4. Trustees

Q3. Which statement best describes “Arbitrage fund”?

  1. Gold is held as a hedge against inflation and uncertainty; it can be owned through physical gold, gold ETFs, sovereign gold bonds or gold funds.
  2. A closed-ended scheme has a fixed maturity and units are bought during the NFO only; investors exit at maturity or by selling on a stock exchange where listed.
  3. An arbitrage fund invests at least 65% in equity and equity-related instruments and profits from price differences between cash and futures markets; it is taxed as equity.
  4. Mutual fund units can be held in dematerialised form with a depository participant, in addition to a statement of account from the AMC.

Q4. Which term is described here? A large cap fund invests at least 80% of its assets in the top 100 companies by market capitalisation.

  1. XIRR
  2. Credit risk
  3. Credit risk fund
  4. Large cap fund

Q5. Which statement best describes “Sectoral/thematic fund”?

  1. A change of bank details needs a written request with proof of the new bank and is checked by the RTA; some schemes impose a cooling-off for large redemptions.
  2. A sectoral or thematic fund invests at least 80% in a particular sector or theme, such as banking or technology, and carries concentration risk.
  3. The cut-off time is the time of day by which a valid application with funds must be received to get that day's NAV. For most schemes it is 3:00 pm.
  4. A large cap fund invests at least 80% of its assets in the top 100 companies by market capitalisation.

Q6. Which statement best describes “Flexi cap fund”?

  1. A flexi cap fund invests at least 65% in equity with no restriction on market-cap allocation, so the manager can move across large, mid and small cap.
  2. Investors can file complaints against AMCs and intermediaries through SEBI's complaint system, and use the online dispute resolution platform if not satisfied.
  3. Liquidity risk is the chance a fund cannot sell securities quickly without a price discount, such as in thinly traded bonds.
  4. In India a mutual fund is constituted as a public trust under the Indian Trusts Act, and the trust is registered with SEBI.
