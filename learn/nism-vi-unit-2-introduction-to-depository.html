Key terms, examples and exam traps

Depository

A depository is an organisation that holds securities of investors in electronic form and facilitates their transfer through book entries.

Example. An investor's shares are held as electronic records instead of paper certificates.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A depository is a stock exchange that lists new companies.

Problems of physical securities

Physical certificates carry risks of loss, theft, forgery, bad delivery and delay in transfer and paperwork.

Example. A certificate lost in transit has to be replaced with effort and cost.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Physical certificates cannot be lost or forged.

Depositories Act, 1996

The Depositories Act, 1996 provides the legal framework for depositories, dematerialisation and the transfer of securities by book entry.

Example. The Act lets securities be held and transferred without certificates.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The Act applies only to physical securities.

NSDL and CDSL

India has two depositories, National Securities Depository Limited (NSDL) and Central Depository Services (India) Limited (CDSL).

Example. An investor may open her demat account with a DP linked to either depository.

Exam trap. This statement is false: India has a single depository for all securities.

Beneficial owner

The beneficial owner (BO) is the person whose name appears in the depository's records as the owner of the securities and who enjoys their benefits.

Example. The investor receives dividends and votes on her demat shares.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The depository enjoys the benefits of securities held by BOs.

Registered owner

In the issuer's books the depository appears as the registered owner of dematerialised securities but holds them for the beneficial owner.

Example. The company's register shows NSDL as holder of shares held in demat form.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The registered owner has voting rights over the shares.

Fungibility

Securities held in a depository are fungible, so no distinguishing numbers or certificate marks are kept for individual securities.

Example. Any 10 shares of a company are equal to another 10 shares of the same company.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Each demat share keeps its own distinctive number.

Benefits of the depository system

Holding in demat form saves time, avoids bad delivery, reduces paperwork, allows trading in single-share lots and speeds up transfer.

Example. An investor can sell a single share without any certificate.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Demat holding makes transfers slower than physical holding.

Depository versus bank

A depository holds securities of investors just as a bank holds their money, and transfers move by book entry.

Example. Shares move from one demat account to another like money between bank accounts.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A depository pays interest on securities held with it.
