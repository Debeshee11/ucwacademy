Category I, II and III AIFs, who can invest, fee structures, lock-ins and the risks of private pooled funds.

Key ideas you will take away

  • AIFs are privately pooled and SEBI-regulated.
  • Minimum ₹1 crore.
  • Cat I: start-ups, infrastructure.
  • Cat II: private equity and debt.
  • Commitment is paid in drawdowns.
  • Tenure often runs several years.

Try a question

Q1. Which statement best describes “Alternative Investment Fund”?

  1. An AIF is a privately pooled investment vehicle regulated by SEBI, collecting money from sophisticated investors to invest under a defined strategy.
  2. SEBI sets the minimum investment in an AIF at ₹1 crore per investor, with exceptions for certain managers’ employees.
  3. Carried interest, or carry, is the manager’s share of profits above the hurdle rate, often around 20%.
  4. The tenure is the life of the fund, during which money is locked in. SEBI requires a minimum tenure of three years for Category I and II funds.
Show the answer

A. Alternative Investment Fund: An AIF is a privately pooled investment vehicle regulated by SEBI, collecting money from sophisticated investors to invest under a defined strategy.

Q2. Which statement best describes “Category I AIF”?

  1. SEBI sets the minimum investment in an AIF at ₹1 crore per investor, with exceptions for certain managers’ employees.
  2. A distribution is a payment returned to investors when the fund sells an investment or earns income.
  3. An AIF is a privately pooled investment vehicle regulated by SEBI, collecting money from sophisticated investors to invest under a defined strategy.
  4. Category I AIFs invest in start-ups, early-stage ventures, social ventures, SMEs and infrastructure that the government wants to encourage.
Show the answer
