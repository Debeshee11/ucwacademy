Key terms, examples and exam traps

Risk management process

Risk management is identifying, measuring, monitoring and controlling risks relative to the portfolio's objectives.

Example. A manager sets limits and checks them daily.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Risk management means avoiding all risk.

Standard deviation

Standard deviation measures how widely returns vary around their average and is the common measure of total risk.

Example. A higher standard deviation means a more volatile fund.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Standard deviation measures only downside.

Value at Risk

VaR estimates the largest loss expected over a period at a stated confidence level, under normal conditions.

Example. A one-day 95% VaR of ₹2 lakh.

Exam trap. This statement is false: VaR is the maximum possible loss.

Maximum drawdown

Maximum drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough fall in value over a period, showing the worst loss experienced.

Example. A fund falls from ₹120 to ₹90 before recovering.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Drawdown is the average annual loss.

Tracking error

Tracking error is the standard deviation of the difference between portfolio and benchmark returns, measuring active risk.

Example. A fund with a tracking error of 3% deviates from its index.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Tracking error measures market risk.

Liquidity risk

Liquidity risk is the risk of being unable to sell a position quickly without a large price concession.

Example. A manager cannot exit a small-cap holding without moving the price.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Liquidity risk applies only to bonds.

Concentration risk

Concentration risk arises when a large part of the portfolio is in a few securities or sectors.

Example. A portfolio holds 40% in one sector.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Concentration reduces risk.

Counterparty risk

Counterparty risk is the risk that the other party to a contract fails to meet its obligation.

Example. A derivative contract is traded over the counter.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Exchange-traded trades carry no counterparty protection.

Stress testing

Stress testing evaluates portfolio losses under extreme but plausible scenarios, complementing statistical measures.

Example. A manager tests a 30% market fall.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Stress tests use only normal markets.

Operational risk

Operational risk is the risk of loss from failed processes, people, systems or external events, including errors and fraud.

Example. A trade is mistakenly entered twice.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Operational risk arises only from market moves.

Risk limits

Risk limits, such as caps on single-stock and sector exposure, keep the portfolio within the client's agreed risk profile.

Example. A manager limits a stock to 10%.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Limits are set after losses occur.
