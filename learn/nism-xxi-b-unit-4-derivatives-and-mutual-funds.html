Key terms, examples and exam traps

Derivative

A derivative is a contract whose value is derived from an underlying asset, index or rate.

Example. A futures contract on an index.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A derivative has its own value independent of any underlying.

Futures contract

A futures contract is a standardised agreement to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a future date, with daily settlement.

Example. A manager sells index futures to cover a falling market.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A futures contract gives only the right, not the obligation, to trade.

Option

An option gives the buyer the right but not the obligation to buy or sell at a strike price, for a premium paid to the seller.

Example. A put option protects a holding against a fall.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The option buyer must always exercise.

Hedging

Hedging uses derivatives to reduce an existing risk, such as selling futures against a stock portfolio.

Example. A manager hedges a portfolio before an election.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Hedging always raises expected return.

Protective put

A protective put combines a long stock holding with a bought put to set a floor on loss.

Example. A holder buys puts and limits downside.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A protective put removes the cost of insurance.

Covered call

A covered call is a long stock holding with a call sold against it, earning premium but capping upside.

Example. A holder writes calls to earn extra income.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A covered call has unlimited upside.

Margin

Exchange-traded derivatives require margin as a deposit that covers possible losses, and it is adjusted with price moves.

Example. A trader posts margin when buying futures.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Margin is a fee that is never refunded.

Mutual fund structure

A mutual fund is a trust that pools investor money, run by an AMC, with a trustee and a custodian providing oversight and safekeeping.

Example. Investors hold units in a pooled trust.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A mutual fund is a company owned by its AMC.

NAV

NAV is the value of a fund's assets less liabilities divided by the units outstanding.

Example. A fund with ₹100 crore of net assets and 10 crore units has NAV of ₹10.

Exam trap. This statement is false: NAV is set by the fund's demand in the market.

Expense ratio

The expense ratio is the annual cost of running a fund, charged as a percentage of assets and reducing returns.

Example. A fund charges 1% a year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Expenses are charged only when the fund makes a profit.

Derivatives in portfolios

Portfolio managers may use derivatives for hedging, portfolio rebalancing and as permitted by the agreement and regulations, with disclosure of the approach.

Example. A manager discloses a hedging approach to clients.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Derivatives may be used freely without disclosure.
