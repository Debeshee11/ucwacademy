Key terms, examples and exam traps

Portfolio manager

A portfolio manager is a SEBI-registered body corporate that manages or administers a client's securities or funds under a contract.

Example. A registered firm invests a client's money under a signed agreement.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A portfolio manager can be an unregistered individual.

Discretionary PMS

In discretionary PMS the manager takes investment decisions independently on behalf of the client, without asking approval for each trade.

Example. A manager buys and sells for the client based on the strategy without consent.

Exam trap. This statement is false: In discretionary PMS the client approves every trade.

Non-discretionary PMS

In non-discretionary PMS the manager advises and executes only after the client's prior approval for each decision.

Example. A manager proposes a trade and executes it after the client agrees.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The manager decides without consulting the client.

Advisory PMS

In advisory PMS the manager only advises; the client decides and executes.

Example. The client acts on the manager's recommendation herself.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The advisory manager executes trades for the client.

Minimum investment

A client's minimum investment with a portfolio manager is ₹50 lakh, in funds or securities, with a lower threshold for accredited investors.

Example. A client must commit at least ₹50 lakh.

Exam trap. This statement is false: PMS accepts investments of ₹5 lakh from retail clients.

Investment approach

An investment approach is a distinct style or strategy offered by a portfolio manager, such as large-cap growth or multi-cap value, with defined objectives and risks.

Example. A manager offers a quality-focused multi-cap approach.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An investment approach is a promise of returns.

Client's own account

Securities in a PMS are held in a separate demat account in the client's name, giving ownership and transparency.

Example. A client's shares appear in her own demat account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: PMS pools all client money into one demat account.

Distributor's role

A PMS distributor introduces the service to clients, explains the product, assesses suitability and receives commission as per regulations.

Example. A certified distributor evaluates whether a client fits a PMS.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A distributor manages the client's portfolio.

Direct onboarding

Clients have the option to be onboarded directly with the portfolio manager without a distributor, and the manager must offer a direct plan.

Example. A client approaches the manager directly and avoids distribution commission.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Clients can invest in PMS only through distributors.

Registration of portfolio managers

Portfolio managers must be registered with SEBI and meet net-worth requirements; the minimum net worth is ₹5 crore.

Example. A firm with insufficient net worth cannot be registered.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Portfolio managers need no minimum net worth.

Investor charter

Portfolio managers must provide an investor charter explaining the rights and obligations of clients and the grievance process.

Example. A client receives a document describing her rights.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Investor charters are optional.

Segregation of client assets

Client assets must be kept separate from the manager's own assets, with a custodian required for safekeeping.

Example. A custodian holds the securities of PMS clients separately.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A manager may hold client assets alongside its own.

Limits on pooling

A portfolio manager cannot pool client funds the way a mutual fund does; each client's portfolio is managed individually.

Example. Each client's holdings are separate in a PMS account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: PMS invests all client money in a single pool.
