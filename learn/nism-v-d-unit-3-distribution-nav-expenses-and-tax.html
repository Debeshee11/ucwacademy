Key terms, examples and exam traps

Net asset value

NAV is the net assets of a scheme after liabilities divided by units outstanding, and is the price at which units are bought and sold.

Example. A scheme with ₹100 crore net assets and 5 crore units has a NAV of ₹20.

Exam trap. This statement is false: NAV is the market price set by supply and demand.

Total expense ratio

TER is the annual cost of running a scheme as a percentage of assets, charged to the scheme and reflected in NAV.

Example. A 1% TER reduces the return the investor sees by 1% a year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: TER is paid by the investor separately at purchase.

Direct and regular plans

A direct plan has no distributor commission and a lower TER, while a regular plan includes distributor commission in its expenses.

Example. A client buying through a distributor holds the regular plan.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A direct plan costs more than a regular plan.

Exit load

Exit load is a charge deducted on redemption within a stated period and goes to the scheme, reducing the amount the investor receives.

Example. A 1% exit load on redemption within one year reduces the proceeds.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Exit load is charged on all units regardless of holding period.

Cut-off time

Applications for liquid funds and others are allotted the NAV of the day only if received before the cut-off time, otherwise the next day's NAV applies.

Example. A purchase after the cut-off time gets the next day's NAV.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Any time of the day gets the same day's NAV.

Stamp duty on units

Stamp duty is charged at 0.005% on purchase of units, including switches and SIP instalments.

Example. A small deduction appears at allotment.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Stamp duty is charged only on redemption.

Equity fund taxation

Gains on equity-oriented funds are short-term if sold within 12 months, taxed at 20%, and long-term above that, taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh in a year (as at writing).

Example. An investor with ₹3 lakh long-term gain pays tax on the amount above ₹1.25 lakh.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Equity fund gains are tax-free.

Taxation depends on portfolio

How a fund is taxed depends on the nature of its underlying portfolio, so distributors must check the scheme's tax treatment from its documents before advising.

Example. A distributor checks how the scheme is classified for tax.

Exam trap. This statement is false: All schemes are taxed alike.

Distributor commission

AMCs pay trail commission to distributors for regular plans and this must be disclosed to investors.

Example. A distributor tells the client how she is paid.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Commission is hidden from the investor.
