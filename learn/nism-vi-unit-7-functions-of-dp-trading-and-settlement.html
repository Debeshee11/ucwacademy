Key terms, examples and exam traps

Settlement cycle

Equity trades on exchanges settle on a T+1 rolling basis, meaning one working day after the trade day.

Example. A trade made on Tuesday settles on Wednesday.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Equity trades settle on T+5.

Delivery instruction slip

The BO gives a signed delivery instruction slip (DIS), issued by the DP in a booklet with pre-printed serial numbers, to debit securities from her account.

Example. A client submits a DIS to deliver shares for a sale.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A DIS is used to credit securities to an account.

Electronic delivery instruction

BOs can give delivery instructions online using e-DIS authorised by a TPIN and one-time password.

Example. A client authorises delivery through the depository's online facility.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Online instructions need no authorisation.

On-market transfer

An on-market transfer settles a trade executed on a stock exchange, with delivery through the clearing corporation.

Example. A seller's securities move to the clearing corporation for settlement of her trade.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An on-market transfer happens only between two relatives.

Off-market transfer

An off-market transfer is a transfer of securities directly between two BOs without a trade on an exchange, such as a gift or family transfer.

Example. A father transfers shares to his daughter's account directly.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An off-market transfer settles through the stock exchange's clearing corporation.

Inter-depository transfer

An inter-depository transfer moves securities between accounts held with different depositories.

Example. A holder shifts shares from an NSDL account to a CDSL account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Securities cannot be moved between NSDL and CDSL accounts.

Pay-in and pay-out

Pay-in is the delivery of securities to the clearing corporation by sellers, and pay-out is the delivery of securities from it to buyers.

Example. The seller's DP delivers shares at pay-in and the buyer receives them at pay-out.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Pay-in is the delivery of securities to the buyer.

Early pay-in

Securities may be delivered at the usual pay-in time or earlier, called early pay-in, and the DP processes instructions before the deadline.

Example. A seller delivers shares a day before the settlement day.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Delivery can be made only on the settlement day itself.

Failed instructions

An instruction fails if securities are insufficient, the account is frozen or details are wrong, and the DP must tell the BO or correct it.

Example. A delivery fails because the account holds fewer shares than needed.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A failed delivery is carried out anyway.

Freeze or suspension

A BO or an authority can freeze an account or particular securities, with a debit freeze stopping outgoing transfers and a total freeze stopping both debits and credits.

Example. A debit freeze stops sales but allows credits.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A debit freeze blocks both credits and debits.

Settlement guarantee and risk

The clearing corporation guarantees the settlement of exchange trades, which lowers counterparty risk.

Example. The buyer is protected if the seller defaults on delivery.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Settlement of exchange trades is not guaranteed by anyone.
