Key terms, examples and exam traps

Mutual fund

A mutual fund pools money from many investors into a scheme managed by an AMC, with investors owning units.

Example. Investors hold units in a pooled scheme.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A mutual fund holds each investor's securities separately.

ETF

An ETF is a fund traded on exchanges, typically tracking an index at low cost.

Example. An investor buys units of a fund like a share.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An ETF is bought only from the AMC at NAV.

Alternative investment fund

An AIF is a privately pooled vehicle for sophisticated investors with a minimum of ₹1 crore.

Example. An investor commits ₹1 crore to a private equity fund.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An AIF accepts small retail investments.

PMS versus mutual fund

In PMS, securities are held in the client's own account and each trade is tax-relevant for the client; in mutual funds, investors hold pooled units.

Example. A PMS client's demat shows the underlying shares.

Exam trap. This statement is false: In PMS, assets are pooled with other clients' money.

NAV

NAV is the per-unit value of a scheme, equal to net assets divided by units outstanding.

Example. A scheme's value per unit is ₹40.

Exam trap. This statement is false: NAV is used to value a PMS client's individual holdings.

Expense versus fee

Mutual funds charge an expense ratio inside NAV, while PMS charges management and performance fees separately to the client.

Example. A PMS client is billed management fee and performance fee.

Exam trap. This statement is false: PMS has no fees.

Transparency

PMS clients see their own holdings and transactions, while mutual fund investors see a pooled portfolio.

Example. A PMS client checks each stock in her own portfolio.

Exam trap. This statement is false: PMS clients cannot see holdings.
