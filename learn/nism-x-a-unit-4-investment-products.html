Key terms, examples and exam traps

Fixed deposit

A fixed deposit gives a fixed return for a fixed period, with capital safety up to the deposit insurance limit and interest taxable at slab rates.

Example. A saver locks a sum for three years at a known rate.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A fixed deposit gives market-linked returns.

Deposit insurance

Deposit insurance covers bank deposits up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank in case of bank failure.

Example. A depositor holds ₹8 lakh in one bank, with ₹5 lakh insured.

Exam trap. This statement is false: All deposits of any amount are fully insured.

Public Provident Fund

PPF is a government-backed small savings scheme with a 15-year tenure, tax benefits on deposit and interest, and limited premature withdrawal.

Example. A long-term saver builds a tax-efficient corpus over fifteen years.

Exam trap. This statement is false: PPF can be fully withdrawn after one year.

Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana

The Sukanya Samriddhi account is a small savings scheme for the girl child with tax benefits and a long lock-in until maturity or marriage after 18.

Example. A parent opens an account for her daughter's education and marriage.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Sukanya Samriddhi is available for any child.

Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme

SCSS is a government-backed scheme for those aged 60 and above with a five-year tenure, offering regular interest payouts.

Example. A retiree places a lump sum for regular quarterly income.

Exam trap. This statement is false: SCSS is open to any age.

Sovereign gold bond

A sovereign gold bond is a government security denominated in grams of gold that pays interest and has no storage or purity issues, with a defined tenure.

Example. An investor holds gold on paper, backed by the government, and earns annual interest.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A sovereign gold bond pays no interest.

Gold ETF

A gold ETF is a fund that holds physical gold and trades on an exchange, giving gold exposure without storage.

Example. An investor buys units of gold in a demat account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A gold ETF offers jewellery on redemption.

Equity share

An equity share offers ownership and growth potential, dividends and voting rights, but with the highest risk among common products.

Example. A client wants long-term growth and can bear volatility.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Equity gives assured returns.

REIT

A REIT owns and operates income-producing real estate and distributes most of its income to unit holders, listed on exchanges.

Example. An investor holds units in a trust that owns office buildings.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A REIT invests in debt instruments of companies.

InvIT

An InvIT pools money to invest in infrastructure assets such as roads and power lines and distributes income to unit holders.

Example. An investor buys units in a trust that owns toll roads.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An InvIT invests only in residential flats.

Real estate

Real estate offers appreciation and rental income but is illiquid, high in ticket size and costly to transact.

Example. A client finds it hard to sell a property quickly.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Real estate is a liquid investment.

PMS

Portfolio management services manage a client's portfolio of securities in a segregated account, with a minimum investment of ₹50 lakh.

Example. A wealthy client hands over a securities portfolio to a professional manager.

Exam trap. This statement is false: PMS has no minimum investment.

Alternative investment fund

An AIF is a privately pooled fund investing in non-traditional assets and strategies, with a minimum investment of ₹1 crore.

Example. An investor puts ₹1 crore into a private equity fund.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An AIF accepts investments of ₹10,000.

Structured product

A structured product combines a debt instrument with a derivative to deliver a customised payoff, and may carry high complexity and issuer risk.

Example. A product offers returns linked to an index with partial capital protection.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Structured products are simple and risk free.

NPS as a product

NPS is a defined-contribution pension scheme invested in equity, corporate bonds and government securities, with a partly mandatory annuity at exit.

Example. A person builds a retirement corpus with regulated pension fund managers.

Exam trap. This statement is false: NPS is a mutual fund.

Bonds and debentures

Bonds are debt instruments paying interest and returning principal at maturity; yields depend on credit quality and tenure.

Example. A client receives coupons and principal on a corporate bond.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Bond returns are linked to company profits.

Annuity

An annuity is a series of regular payments, usually from an insurer, bought with a lump sum, to provide income in retirement.

Example. A retiree pays a lump sum and receives monthly income for life.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An annuity pays a single lump sum at maturity.

Unit linked insurance plan

A ULIP combines insurance with market-linked investment, with charges for mortality, administration and fund management.

Example. A policy invests part of the premium in funds and part in cover.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A ULIP has no charges.

Corporate fixed deposit

A corporate fixed deposit is a deposit with a company at a higher rate than banks, with higher risk and no deposit insurance.

Example. A client considers a company deposit for its higher interest.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Company deposits are insured like bank deposits.

Recurring deposit

A recurring deposit lets a saver deposit a fixed sum every month for a fixed term, earning a fixed interest rate.

Example. A saver puts ₹5,000 monthly into a bank for two years.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A recurring deposit needs a single lump sum at the start.
