Key terms, examples and exam traps

Call option buyer

The buyer of a call pays a premium and has the right to buy the underlying at the strike. Maximum loss is the premium and the potential profit is unlimited.

Example. A trader pays ₹20 for the right to buy at ₹500 and expects a rise.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A call buyer has unlimited loss.

Call option seller

The call seller (writer) receives the premium and must sell the underlying at the strike if exercised. The maximum profit is the premium and the loss is potentially unlimited.

Example. A trader receives ₹20 and must sell if the price rises sharply.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A call writer has unlimited profit and limited loss.

Put option buyer

The buyer of a put pays a premium and has the right to sell at the strike. Maximum loss is the premium; profit rises as the price falls to zero.

Example. A trader pays ₹15 for the right to sell at ₹500 and expects a fall.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A put buyer benefits when the price rises.

Put option seller

The put seller receives the premium and must buy the underlying at the strike if exercised. Maximum profit is the premium; loss can be large if prices fall.

Example. A trader receives ₹15 and must buy at ₹500 if the price collapses.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A put writer has unlimited profit.

In-the-money

A call is in the money when spot is above the strike; a put is in the money when spot is below the strike. It has positive intrinsic value.

Example. A call with strike 500 when spot is 550.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A call is in the money when spot is below the strike.

At-the-money

An option is at the money when the strike equals the spot price. Its intrinsic value is zero.

Example. A call with strike 500 when spot is 500.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An at-the-money option has high intrinsic value.

Out-of-the-money

A call is out of the money when spot is below the strike; a put when spot is above the strike. It has no intrinsic value, only time value.

Example. A put with strike 500 when spot is 550.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An out-of-the-money option has the highest intrinsic value.

Intrinsic value

Intrinsic value is the amount by which an option is in the money, and is never negative. For a call it is the greater of (spot − strike) and zero.

Example. A call with strike 500 and spot 560 has a value of ₹60 on exercise.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Intrinsic value of an option can be negative.

Time value

Time value is the part of the premium above intrinsic value, reflecting the chance of further favourable moves; it falls to zero at expiry.

Example. An option with intrinsic value ₹60 and premium ₹75 has ₹15 of this.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Time value rises as expiry approaches.

Option premium determinants

The premium depends on spot, strike, time to expiry, volatility, interest rates and dividends; higher volatility and longer time raise it.

Example. An option on a very volatile stock costs more than one on a stable stock.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Volatility has no effect on option premium.

Implied volatility

Implied volatility is the volatility the market's option prices imply for the underlying, derived from the option price.

Example. Option prices suggest the market expects larger swings.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Implied volatility is the volatility in the past year.

Delta

Delta measures the change in an option's price for a ₹1 change in the underlying. Calls have delta between 0 and 1; puts between −1 and 0.

Example. A call with delta 0.5 gains about ₹0.50 if the underlying rises ₹1.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Delta measures the effect of time decay.

Gamma

Gamma measures how much delta changes for a ₹1 move in the underlying. It is highest for at-the-money options near expiry.

Example. The sensitivity of delta is greatest for options near the strike.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Gamma is the change in price for time decay.

Theta

Theta measures the loss in option value as one day passes, other things equal. It hurts option buyers and helps sellers.

Example. An option loses a little value each day as expiry nears.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Theta helps option buyers.

Vega

Vega measures the change in an option's price for a 1% change in volatility. Higher volatility raises both call and put prices.

Example. An option's price rises when volatility increases.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Vega measures the effect of interest rate changes.

Rho

Rho measures the change in an option's price for a 1% change in the interest rate.

Example. An option's sensitivity to the interest rate.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Rho measures the effect of volatility on price.

European option

A European option can be exercised only on the expiry date. All options on Indian exchanges are European style.

Example. A holder can exercise an option only on the last day.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A European option can be exercised any time before expiry.

American option

An American option can be exercised at any time up to and including expiry.

Example. A holder can exercise whenever she chooses before expiry.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An American option can be exercised only at expiry.

Exercise and assignment

When a buyer exercises, a seller is assigned at random or by rule and must perform the obligation under the contract.

Example. A call buyer exercises and a writer is called to deliver.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The option buyer is always obliged to exercise.

Settlement of index options

Index options are cash settled at expiry on the closing index value; in-the-money options are exercised automatically.

Example. An in-the-money index option ends with a cash credit.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Index options are settled by delivering all index shares.

Settlement of stock options

Stock options are physically settled; in-the-money stock options at expiry result in delivery of shares.

Example. A call buyer who is in the money at expiry gets shares delivered.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Stock options are always cash settled.

Put-call parity

For European options, the call price minus the put price equals the present value relationship of spot and strike; deviations offer arbitrage.

Example. A call and put with the same strike and expiry are linked by spot and strike.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Calls and puts are priced independently.

Long call payoff

A long call's profit at expiry = max(spot − strike, 0) − premium; break-even = strike + premium.

Example. A call with strike 500 bought at ₹20 breaks even at 520.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A long call's break-even is strike minus premium.

Long put payoff

A long put's profit at expiry = max(strike − spot, 0) − premium; break-even = strike − premium.

Example. A put with strike 500 bought at ₹15 breaks even at 485.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A long put's break-even is strike plus premium.

Short call payoff

A short call's profit at expiry = premium − max(spot − strike, 0); break-even = strike + premium.

Example. A writer who sold a 500 call at ₹20 starts losing above 520.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A short call is profitable at any rise in price.

Short put payoff

A short put's profit at expiry = premium − max(strike − spot, 0); break-even = strike − premium.

Example. A writer who sold a 500 put at ₹15 loses below 485.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A short put is profitable at any fall in price.

Option writer margin

Option sellers must deposit margin because of their potential large losses; option buyers pay only the premium and need no margin.

Example. A seller deposits margin while the buyer only pays the premium.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Option buyers must deposit margin like sellers.

Premium settlement

Premium is paid by the buyer and received by the seller on the next working day after the trade.

Example. The premium for an option traded today is settled the following business day.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Premium is paid only at expiry.

Option chain

An option chain lists the available strikes and premiums of calls and puts for a given underlying and expiry.

Example. A table showing calls on one side and puts on the other by strike price.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An option chain shows only futures prices.

Weekly options

Exchanges list options with weekly expiries on selected indices, subject to regulatory limits on the number of contracts.

Example. Some index options expire every week.

Exam trap. This statement is false: All stock options expire weekly.

Early exercise of European options

Because European options cannot be exercised early, holders who want to exit before expiry sell the option in the market.

Example. A holder sells the option in the market a week before expiry.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A European option holder may exercise any day.
