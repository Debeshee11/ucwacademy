Key ideas you will take away

  • A share is ownership, not a loan.
  • Exchanges match buyers and sellers; SEBI regulates.
  • Demat = electronic locker for shares.
  • Limit orders control price, not certainty.
  • Settlement is T+1.
  • The bid-ask spread is a cost.

Try a question

Q1. Which statement best describes “Share”?

  1. SEBI is India’s securities market regulator. It protects investors, registers intermediaries and writes the rules for exchanges and brokers.
  2. A share is a unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder owns a small part of the business and shares in its profits and risks.
  3. An initial public offering is when a company sells shares to the public for the first time to raise money, and then lists on an exchange.
  4. A stock exchange is a regulated marketplace where buyers and sellers trade listed shares at transparent prices. India’s main exchanges are NSE and BSE.
Show the answer

B. Share: A share is a unit of ownership in a company. A shareholder owns a small part of the business and shares in its profits and risks.

Q2. Which statement best describes “Demat account”?

  1. A stock split divides each share into more shares at a lower price. The number of shares rises but the total value you own is unchanged.
  2. A demat account holds your shares in electronic form, like a bank account holds money. The depositories NSDL and CDSL keep the records.
  3. A stop-loss order triggers a sale when the price falls to a level you set, to cap the loss on a position. In a fast fall it may fill below that level.
  4. A trading account is the account through which you place buy and sell orders. It connects your bank account and your demat account.
Show the answer

B. Demat account: A demat account holds your shares in electronic form, like a bank account holds money. The depositories NSDL and CDSL keep the records.
