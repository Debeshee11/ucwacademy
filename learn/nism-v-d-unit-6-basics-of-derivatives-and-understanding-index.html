Key terms, examples and exam traps

Derivative

A derivative is a contract whose value is derived from an underlying asset such as a share, index, currency or interest rate.

Example. An index future derives its value from the index.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A derivative has a value independent of any underlying.

Hedger

A hedger uses derivatives to reduce an existing risk.

Example. A fund holding shares sells index futures to protect against a fall.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A hedger takes derivative positions to increase risk.

Speculator

A speculator takes a view on price movements and accepts risk in hope of profit.

Example. A trader buys futures expecting the index to rise.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A speculator uses derivatives to remove risk.

Arbitrageur

An arbitrageur exploits price differences between related markets to earn riskless profit.

Example. A trader buys in the cash market and sells in the futures market when they are mispriced.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An arbitrageur depends on the market direction.

Types of derivatives

The main types are forwards, futures, options and swaps.

Example. A fund uses futures and options on an index.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Shares and bonds are derivatives.

Exchange-traded and OTC

Exchange-traded derivatives are standardised and cleared through a clearing corporation, while OTC derivatives are customised and privately negotiated.

Example. Index futures trade on an exchange.

Exam trap. This statement is false: OTC derivatives are traded on exchange with standard terms.

Underlying

The underlying is the asset, rate or index from which the derivative gets its value.

Example. An option on the Nifty has the Nifty index as the underlying.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The underlying is the derivative contract itself.

Stock index

A stock index measures the value of a basket of shares to show the market's movement.

Example. The Nifty 50 tracks 50 large stocks.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An index tracks a single stock.

Free-float market capitalisation

Index weights are commonly based on free-float market capitalisation, which counts only the shares available for public trading.

Example. A company with a large promoter holding gets a lower weight than its full market cap.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Free-float counts shares held by promoters.

Index uses

Indices are used as benchmarks, for index funds and ETFs and as the underlying for futures and options.

Example. A fund compares itself with its benchmark index.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Indices cannot be used for investing.

Systematic and unsystematic risk

Systematic risk affects the whole market and cannot be diversified away, while unsystematic risk is specific to a company and can be diversified.

Example. An index future hedges market risk.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Index futures hedge company-specific risk completely.
