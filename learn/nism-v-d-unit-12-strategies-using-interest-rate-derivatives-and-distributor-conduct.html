Key terms, examples and exam traps

Hedging a bond portfolio

A holder of bonds fears a rise in yields and hedges by selling interest rate futures to offset the fall in bond values.

Example. A fund sells G-Sec futures against its bond holdings.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A holder of bonds hedges by buying more bonds.

Adjusting duration

A manager can lengthen or shorten a portfolio's effective duration using interest rate futures without selling bonds.

Example. A manager reduces duration using short futures.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Duration can be changed only by selling bonds.

Trading a view on rates

A trader expecting yields to fall buys interest rate futures and one expecting yields to rise sells them.

Example. A trader buys futures before an expected rate cut.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A trader expecting a rate cut sells futures.

Disclosure of complexity

Distributors must explain the strategy, derivative use and risks of a SIF in plain language before the investor decides.

Example. A distributor explains how short positions can lose money.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A distributor should only mention likely gains.

No assured returns

Distributors must not promise or imply guaranteed returns on mutual funds or SIFs.

Example. A distributor refuses to state a fixed return for a SIF.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A distributor may promise 12% for a SIF.

Code of conduct

Distributors must act with honesty and care, avoid mis-selling, keep client information confidential and disclose commissions.

Example. A distributor discloses the commission and gives true information.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The code allows exaggerated claims.

Mis-selling

Mis-selling is selling a product that does not suit the client or by giving misleading information, and it can lead to penalties.

Example. A distributor sells a complex SIF to a client who needs regular liquidity.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Mis-selling happens only if the client loses money.

Grievance redressal

Investors can complain to the AMC, then to SEBI's complaint system and use online dispute resolution if unresolved.

Example. An unhappy investor files a complaint on SCORES.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Investors have no place to complain about mis-selling.
