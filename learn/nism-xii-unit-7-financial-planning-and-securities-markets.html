Key terms, examples and exam traps

Financial planning

Financial planning is the process of setting goals and arranging income, savings, investments, insurance and tax to meet them.

Example. A family plans funds for a house, education and retirement.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Financial planning means only buying shares.

Financial goal

A financial goal is a specific need with an amount and a time frame, such as a child's education in 12 years.

Example. A couple wants ₹30 lakh for education in ten years.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Goals need no amount or deadline.

Risk profile

A risk profile combines a person's ability and willingness to take risk, guiding the choice of investments.

Example. An investor who cannot sleep after a small loss has a low willingness to take risk.

Exam trap. This statement is false: All investors have the same risk profile.

Asset allocation

Asset allocation divides investments among asset classes like equity, debt and gold, according to goals, horizon and risk profile.

Example. A saver holds 60% equity, 30% debt and 10% gold.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Asset allocation means putting everything into one stock.

Time value of money

A rupee today is worth more than a rupee tomorrow because it can be invested to earn a return.

Example. ₹1 lakh invested today grows, so it is worth more than ₹1 lakh in five years.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Money has the same value at any time.

Compounding

Compounding means earning returns on both the original amount and the returns accumulated earlier, which accelerates growth over long periods.

Example. Reinvested gains themselves earn further gains.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Compounding means earning returns only on the original amount.

Inflation

Inflation is the sustained rise in the general price level, reducing the purchasing power of money.

Example. A basket of goods costing ₹100 last year costs ₹106 now.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Inflation raises the value of cash.

Emergency fund

An emergency fund is a reserve of liquid funds covering three to six months of expenses for unexpected needs.

Example. A family keeps six months of expenses in a savings account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An emergency fund should be invested in small-cap stocks.

Insurance and investment

Term insurance gives protection against loss of life; it should be separated from investment, which aims at building wealth.

Example. A person buys a term plan for cover and invests separately for growth.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A mutual fund gives life cover.

Life-cycle stages

Different stages of life, such as starting a career, family and retirement, change goals, income and the right mix of assets.

Example. A 25-year-old can usually hold more equity than a 60-year-old.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Life stage has no bearing on investments.

Diversification

Diversification spreads money across assets and securities to reduce the impact of any one loss.

Example. An investor holds many companies across sectors instead of one.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Diversification guarantees no loss.

Investor grievance redressal

Investors can complain first to the intermediary, then to the exchange or SEBI's SCORES platform, and use the online dispute resolution route.

Example. A client with an unresolved complaint files it on the regulator's portal.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Investors have no route for complaints.

Ponzi scheme

A Ponzi scheme pays earlier investors with the money of newer ones and promises unusually high, assured returns, and it collapses when new money dries up.

Example. A scheme promises 4% a month and pays old investors from new deposits.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A scheme promising very high assured returns is safe.

Red flags in advice

Promises of guaranteed returns, pressure to invest quickly and unregistered advisers are warning signs of fraud.

Example. A caller promises double returns in three months and asks for urgent payment.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Registered advisers promise assured returns.

Registered intermediary

Investors should deal only with SEBI-registered brokers, advisers and distributors, whose registration can be checked on the regulator's site.

Example. An investor checks an adviser's registration number before engaging.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Registration of intermediaries is unnecessary.

Tax on investments

Tax treatment differs across securities and holding periods, so after-tax returns matter in planning.

Example. Two investments with the same gain leave different amounts after tax.

Exam trap. This statement is false: All gains are taxed in the same way.

Q3. Which statement best describes “Ponzi scheme”?

  1. A Ponzi scheme pays earlier investors with the money of newer ones and promises unusually high, assured returns, and it collapses when new money dries up.

  1. Asset allocation divides investments among asset classes like equity, debt and gold, according to goals, horizon and risk profile.

  4. Arbitrage involves buying and selling related instruments at the same time to profit from price differences, with little risk.
