Key terms, examples and exam traps

Research analyst

A research analyst studies securities, companies or markets and publishes recommendations, targets or opinions that investors may rely on.

Example. A person who writes a report recommending a share with a target price.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A research analyst executes trades for clients.

Buy-side and sell-side

Sell-side analysts work for brokers and publish research for clients; buy-side analysts work for fund managers and research for their own investment decisions.

Example. An analyst at a mutual fund researches stocks only for the fund's use.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Buy-side analysts publish reports for the public.

Objectivity and independence

Research must be independent, based on facts and free of undue influence from the subject company or trading desks.

Example. An analyst resists pressure to change a rating after the company objects.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An analyst may change a rating at the request of the subject company.
