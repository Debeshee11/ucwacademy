Key terms, examples and exam traps

Primary market

The primary market is where securities are first issued to investors and the issuer receives the proceeds.

Example. An IPO raises funds for a company.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Investors trade with each other in the primary market.

Secondary market

The secondary market is where existing securities trade between investors on exchanges, providing liquidity.

Example. An investor sells shares through a stock exchange.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The company receives funds from secondary market trades.

Stock exchange

A stock exchange is a regulated platform where listed securities trade, such as the BSE and NSE.

Example. Buyers and sellers meet on an electronic platform.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A stock exchange issues shares to the public.

Depository

A depository holds securities in electronic form, enabling transfer by book entry; India has NSDL and CDSL.

Example. A client's shares are held electronically in a demat account.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A depository sets stock prices.

SEBI

SEBI regulates the securities market including portfolio managers, and frames regulations to protect investors.

Example. A portfolio manager must register with the capital-market regulator.

Exam trap. This statement is false: SEBI regulates only banks.

Settlement

Equity trades settle on T+1 through a clearing corporation, which guarantees settlement.

Example. Shares bought today are delivered the next working day.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Equity trades take a week to settle.
