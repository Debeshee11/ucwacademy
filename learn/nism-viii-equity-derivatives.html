NISM Series VIII syllabus, unit by unit

The exam covers 10 units. The percentage is the share of questions each unit usually carries, so spend your time in that proportion.

Unit 1 Basics of derivatives8%

A derivative gets its value from something else: a share, an index, a currency or a commodity. This unit teaches the language, the purposes of derivatives and the differences between types.

  • Forwards versus futures.
  • Hedgers, speculators and arbitrageurs.
  • Exchange-traded versus over-the-counter.
Unit 2 Understanding index2%

Index derivatives are based on stock indices. A short unit on how an index is built.

  • Market capitalisation weighting and free float.
  • Sensex and Nifty 50.
Unit 3 Introduction to forwards and futures25%

The heaviest unit in the exam. Contract specifications, pricing, payoffs, basis, margins and hedging with index futures.

  • Payoffs of long and short futures.
  • Cost of carry pricing.
  • Basis, contango and backwardation.
  • Hedging with index futures.
Unit 4 Introduction to options25%

Options are the second heavy unit. Calls, puts, moneyness, premium, Greeks, put-call parity and the payoff of each position.

  • Payoffs and break-even of calls and puts.
  • Intrinsic value and time value.
  • The Greeks.
  • Settlement and exercise.
Unit 5 Option trading strategies3%

Combining options and underlying positions shapes the risk and reward. A short unit worth three marks.

  • Covered call, protective put, spreads, straddle, strangle.
Unit 6 Introduction to trading systems4%

How orders are placed and matched in the exchange's electronic system.

  • Order types and priority.
  • Trading hours.
  • Position limits and bans.
Unit 7 Introduction to clearing and settlement system13%

After the trade comes the settlement. This unit covers clearing members, the clearing corporation, margins, daily MTM and final settlement.

  • Roles of clearing corporation and clearing members.
  • Margin types and collateral.
  • Settlement of futures and options.
Unit 8 Legal and regulatory environment15%

Who permits derivatives, who regulates them, who is eligible to trade and what safeguards exist. Questions here test exact names and rules.

  • SCRA, SEBI Act and committees.
  • Eligibility of securities and members.
  • Investor protection and risk disclosure.
Unit 9 Accounting and taxation3%

How profits and losses from derivatives are treated for accounts and tax.

  • Income tax treatment of F&O.
  • STT.
Unit 10 Sales practices and investor protection services2%

Good conduct in selling derivatives, from advice to complaints.

  • Suitability, disclosures and ethics.

Q1. Which term is described here? The clearing corporation maintains a fund to cover member defaults, built from contributions of members and the corporation.

  1. Settlement guarantee fund
  2. Leverage
  3. Settlement of stock options
  4. Self-clearing member

Q2. Which statement best describes “Expiry cycle”?

  1. Basis is the difference between the spot price and the futures price. It narrows to zero at expiry as the two prices converge.
  2. Index and stock futures have near-month, next-month and far-month contracts, and a contract expires on the day set by the exchange; on expiry it is cash settled.
  3. Contango is a market in which futures prices are higher than the spot price, normally due to carrying costs.
  4. When a buyer exercises, a seller is assigned at random or by rule and must perform the obligation under the contract.

Q3. Which statement best describes “Protective put”?

  1. A person whose derivatives turnover or losses cross specified thresholds may need to get accounts audited under the tax law.
  2. A protective put buys a put against a held position, setting a floor under the loss at the cost of the premium.
  3. The clearing corporation becomes the counterparty to every trade through novation, guaranteeing settlement and managing risk.
  4. The MWPL caps the total open positions across all traders in a stock's derivatives; when it reaches 95% the stock enters a ban period.

Q4. Which term is described here? A short put's profit at expiry = premium − max(strike − spot, 0); break-even = strike − premium.

  1. SEBI Act
  2. Short put payoff
  3. Reverse cash-and-carry
  4. L.C. Gupta Committee
