Reading financial statements

Read the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement together, and spot weak quality of earnings.

  2. The balance sheet

Key ideas you will take away

  • Revenue is the top line.
  • Margin shows how much is kept.
  • What a company owns equals what it owes plus equity.
  • Working capital funds the daily business.
  • Profit is not cash.
  • Free cash flow comes after capital spending.

Try a question

Q1. Which statement best describes “Revenue”?

  1. This is profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, a rough measure of operating earnings.
  2. This is operating profit as a percentage of revenue.
  3. This is the money a company earns from selling its goods or services in a period.
  4. This is what is left after all expenses, interest and tax, and it belongs to the shareholders.
Show the answer

C. Revenue: Revenue is the money a company earns from selling its goods or services in a period.

Q2. Which statement best describes “Assets”?

  1. This is what is left of the assets after all liabilities are paid, and it belongs to the owners.
  2. This is current assets minus current liabilities, the money tied up in day-to-day operations.
  3. This are the amounts a company owes to others, such as loans, supplier bills and taxes due.
  4. This are the things a company owns or is owed that have value, such as cash, stock, plants and customer dues.
Show the answer

D. Assets: Assets are the things a company owns or is owed that have value, such as cash, stock, plants and customer dues.
