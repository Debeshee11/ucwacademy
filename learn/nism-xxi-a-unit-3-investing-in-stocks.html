Key terms, examples and exam traps

Equity share

An equity share represents ownership in a company with a residual claim on profits and assets.

Example. A client owns part of a listed company.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Equity gives a fixed return.

Market capitalisation

Market capitalisation is share price times shares outstanding, used to classify companies as large, mid or small cap.

Example. A company with 10 crore shares at ₹300 is valued at ₹3,000 crore.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Market cap is the company's debt.

Price-to-earnings ratio

P/E is the share price divided by earnings per share, showing how much investors pay per rupee of earnings.

Example. A stock at ₹300 with EPS of ₹15 has a P/E of 20.

Exam trap. This statement is false: P/E is earnings divided by price.

Price-to-book ratio

P/B compares share price with book value per share, widely used for banks and asset-heavy companies.

Example. A bank at ₹300 with book value of ₹150 trades at 2 times.

Exam trap. This statement is false: P/B compares price with dividend.

Return on equity

ROE is net profit divided by shareholders' equity and measures how effectively equity is used.

Example. A company earns ₹20 crore on equity of ₹100 crore.

Exam trap. This statement is false: ROE is price divided by earnings.

Growth investing

Growth investing seeks companies with above-average earnings growth, often at higher valuations.

Example. A manager buys fast-growing firms with premium multiples.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Growth investors buy only stocks with the lowest P/E.

Value investing

Value investing buys stocks trading below estimated intrinsic value, expecting the price to converge.

Example. A manager buys a good business at a low multiple.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Value investors buy only the most expensive stocks.

Quality investing

Quality investing favours companies with high returns on capital, stable earnings and strong governance.

Example. A manager picks businesses with durable returns and low debt.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Quality investing seeks the most indebted firms.

Large, mid and small caps

Large caps are generally more stable and liquid; small caps offer higher growth potential with more volatility and lower liquidity.

Example. A small-cap stock falls sharply on a bad quarter.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Small caps are less volatile than large caps.

Dividend

A dividend is a share of profit paid to shareholders, which forms part of total return along with price change.

Example. A company pays ₹6 per share to holders.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Dividends are guaranteed every year.

Equity risks

Equity investors face market, company-specific, valuation and liquidity risks, and can lose capital.

Example. A stock falls 40% after a profit warning.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Equity cannot lose capital over short periods.

Sector concentration

Concentrating a portfolio in one sector raises risk because sector-specific shocks hit all holdings together.

Example. A portfolio holding mostly banking stocks falls when banks fall.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Sector concentration lowers portfolio risk.

Corporate governance

Good governance, with independent boards and transparent disclosure, reduces the risk of value loss to minority shareholders.

Example. A manager avoids a company with weak disclosure.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Governance does not affect investment risk.
