Key terms, examples and exam traps

SEBI (Research Analysts) Regulations, 2014

These regulations require research analysts and research entities to register with SEBI and follow rules on qualification, conduct, disclosure and records.

Example. A person who publishes recommendations for the public must hold SEBI registration.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Research analysts need no registration.

Registration and qualification

An individual research analyst must hold the prescribed qualification and the NISM research analyst certification and meet other norms.

Example. A candidate cleared the NISM examination and holds the specified degree.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The NISM certificate is optional for registration.

Research entity

A research entity is a person such as a broker or company that issues research reports and must be registered; it can employ analysts.

Example. A brokerage house publishing reports must be a registered entity.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A research entity is an individual client.

Disclosure of conflicts

A research analyst must disclose any financial interest, ownership or relationship with the subject company in the report.

Example. A report states that the analyst holds shares of the company.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Analysts need not disclose holdings.

Trading restriction around reports

A research analyst must not deal in securities for 30 days before and 5 days after publishing a report on them.

Example. An analyst cannot trade a stock for a period around her report.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An analyst may trade the stock in the hour before publication.

Assured returns prohibited

A research analyst must not provide, or claim to provide, assured or guaranteed returns.

Example. A report says 'This stock will double in a year for sure.'

Exam trap. This statement is false: An analyst may guarantee returns for large clients.

Record keeping

Research analysts must maintain records of research reports, recommendations and the basis for them for at least five years.

Example. An analyst retains her models and reports on file for five years.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Records must be kept for only six months.

Compliance officer

Registered research entities must appoint a compliance officer who monitors compliance and reports to SEBI as required.

Example. A firm assigns a person to monitor adherence to regulations.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A compliance officer is optional.

Chinese wall

A Chinese wall is a set of information barriers that separates research from other business such as investment banking and trading to prevent misuse of sensitive information.

Example. The research desk is kept apart from the banking desk.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A Chinese wall allows free flow of information.

Front running

Front running is trading ahead of a client's order or an upcoming report to profit from its expected impact, and is prohibited.

Example. An analyst buys shares before releasing a strong buy report.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Front running is allowed if disclosed at a later date.

Insider information

An analyst must not use or share unpublished price-sensitive information, which is an offence under insider trading rules.

Example. An analyst learns of a merger from a source and trades on it.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Using inside information is permitted for research analysts.

Client agreement

An analyst providing services to clients must sign an agreement stating the services, fees and terms, and give disclosures.

Example. A client signs a document setting out the services and fees.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A written agreement is unnecessary.

Public appearance disclosures

When an analyst makes a public appearance, she must disclose any conflicts or holdings in the stocks she discusses.

Example. A television analyst states that she owns the stock she is recommending.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Disclosures are required only in written reports.

Prohibited compensation

A research analyst must not accept compensation from a subject company that could influence the opinion, and any compensation must be disclosed.

Example. An analyst refuses payment from the company she is covering.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An analyst may accept gifts from the company with no disclosure.

Grievance and penalties

Violations of the regulations can lead to penalties, suspension or cancellation of registration by SEBI.

Example. A registered analyst whose rules violations are proven faces a regulatory penalty.

Exam trap. This statement is false: SEBI has no power to penalise research analysts.

Recommendation requirements

Each recommendation must be supported by research data and a rating system, and reports must carry the required disclaimers and disclosures.

Example. A report includes disclosures and the basis of the rating.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Reports need not carry any disclaimers.

Associate's trading

The trading restrictions and disclosure duties also apply to an analyst's associates and relatives where specified in the regulations.

Example. The analyst's spouse trading ahead of a report breaches the rule.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Restrictions apply to analysts only, never to relatives.

Rating and price target history

A research entity should keep and show past ratings and price targets, so readers can see the track record.

Example. A report includes a table of previous ratings.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Past ratings are removed from reports.
