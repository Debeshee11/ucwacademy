Key terms, examples and exam traps

Bond price and yield

Bond prices and yields move in opposite directions, so a rise in yields lowers bond prices.

Example. A bond falls in price when yields rise.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Bond prices rise when yields rise.

Coupon

A coupon is the periodic interest payment a bond pays based on its face value and coupon rate.

Example. A 7% bond of ₹100 pays ₹7 a year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A coupon is the bond's market price.

Yield to maturity

Yield to maturity is the return from holding a bond to maturity, with all coupons reinvested at the same rate.

Example. An investor compares bonds by their yields.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Yield to maturity ignores the bond's price.

Government securities

G-Secs are issued by the government and have no credit risk, but they carry interest rate risk.

Example. A 10-year G-Sec falls in value if yields rise.

Exam trap. This statement is false: G-Secs have no interest rate risk.

Duration

Duration measures a bond's sensitivity to interest rate changes, and a longer duration means greater price change for a change in yield.

Example. A bond with a duration of 7 moves more than one with 2.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Duration measures a bond's credit risk.

Interest rate risk

Interest rate risk is the risk that the value of a bond or fund changes because rates change.

Example. A debt fund falls when yields rise.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Interest rate risk is the same as default risk.

Interest rate derivative

An interest rate derivative is a contract whose value depends on an interest rate or a bond price, and it is used to hedge or take a view on rates.

Example. A debt fund hedges a rate rise with a futures contract.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Interest rate derivatives depend on equity prices.

Money market

The money market deals in short-term instruments such as T-bills, commercial paper and call money.

Example. A liquid fund holds T-bills.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The money market deals only in long-term bonds.
