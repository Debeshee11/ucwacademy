Key terms, examples and exam traps

Suitability

A distributor must assess a client's financial position, experience, risk appetite and horizon before recommending an AIF.

Example. A distributor declines to sell an AIF to a client who needs funds soon.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Suitability applies only to mutual funds.

Disclosure to investors

A distributor must give investors the PPM and explain risks, fees, lock-in and illiquidity before they invest.

Example. A distributor explains the lock-in and fees upfront.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Risk factors can be skipped if the client is wealthy.

No guaranteed returns

Neither managers nor distributors may promise or guarantee returns on AIFs, and statements must be fair and balanced.

Example. A distributor must not say, 'This fund will give 20% for sure.'

Exam trap. This statement is false: Distributors may guarantee returns for large clients.

Commission disclosure

Distributors must disclose the commissions they receive and ensure that the client knows direct options exist.

Example. A distributor tells the client about the fee she gets.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Commissions need not be disclosed.

KYC and AML

Investors must complete KYC and anti-money laundering checks, and the source of funds should be verified.

Example. The distributor checks identity and the origin of money.

Exam trap. This statement is false: KYC is waived for large investments.

Conflicts of interest

Distributors must avoid or disclose conflicts, such as higher commission on a product, and put client interest first.

Example. A distributor discloses that one fund pays more and recommends the better fit.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A distributor should recommend the highest-commission fund.

Confidentiality

A distributor must protect client information and share it only with consent or as required by law.

Example. A distributor does not share a client's holdings with others.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Client data may be sold to fund managers.

Record keeping

Distributors should maintain records of recommendations, risk profiling, disclosures and communication for the period required.

Example. A file holds the client's risk profile and the PPM acknowledgement.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Records of AIF sales are optional.
