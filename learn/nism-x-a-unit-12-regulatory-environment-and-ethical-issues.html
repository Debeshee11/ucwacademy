Key terms, examples and exam traps

SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations

These regulations require anyone providing investment advice for a fee to be registered with SEBI and to follow conduct rules.

Example. A person charging a fee for investment advice must hold the regulator's registration.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Anyone can give paid investment advice without registration.

Fee modes

An adviser may charge a fixed fee or a fee based on assets under advice, but not both for the same client, and within the limits prescribed by SEBI.

Example. An adviser charges a flat annual fee rather than a percentage.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Advisers may charge both fixed fee and percentage at the same time.

Fee cap

SEBI has capped fees: up to ₹1.51 lakh per family per annum in fixed fee mode, or up to 2.5% of assets under advice per annum in AUA mode.

Example. A fixed fee cannot exceed the prescribed limit per family.

Exam trap. This statement is false: SEBI places no limit on advisory fees.

Segregation of advisory and distribution

Advisory and distribution must be kept separate: an individual adviser cannot do both for the same client, and non-individual advisers need separate arrangements.

Example. An adviser cannot earn commissions on the products sold to her advisory client.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An adviser may sell commission-based products to her advisory clients.

Risk profiling

Advisers must profile every client's risk before giving advice and document it, to ensure suitability.

Example. An adviser uses a questionnaire to determine the client's risk tolerance.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Risk profiling is optional for advisers.

Suitability

Advice must be suitable for the client's risk profile, goals, horizon and financial position, and the reasons must be recorded.

Example. An adviser recommends a safe product to an elderly client with low risk tolerance.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Advice can ignore the client's risk profile.

Client agreement

An adviser must enter into a written agreement with each client stating fees, services, and the rights and obligations of both.

Example. A client signs a document describing the services and fees.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An adviser can provide services with no written agreement.

Disclosure of conflicts

An adviser must disclose any conflict of interest, such as holdings in recommended securities or relationships with product providers.

Example. An adviser tells the client that she owns the recommended security.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Conflicts need not be disclosed.

Record keeping

An adviser must keep records of advice, risk profile, communications and fees for at least five years.

Example. An adviser retains the client's risk profile and recommendations on file.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Records need to be kept for only one month.

Qualification and certification

An individual adviser needs prescribed qualifications and the NISM certification, and must meet minimum experience and net worth norms.

Example. A person with the right degree and NISM certificate applies for registration.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Certification is not required for registration.

Principal officer and compliance

A non-individual adviser must appoint a principal officer with prescribed qualifications and a compliance officer to ensure compliance.

Example. A firm appoints a certified principal officer.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Firms need not appoint any compliance officer.

Advertisement code

Advertisements by advisers must be fair, not misleading, and must not promise assured returns or guarantee performance.

Example. An adviser's brochure must not claim guaranteed returns.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Advisers may promise guaranteed returns.

Confidentiality

An adviser must keep client information confidential and use it only for the advisory purpose, unless required by law.

Example. An adviser does not share a client's holdings with anyone else.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An adviser may sell client data to product companies.

Code of conduct

The code of conduct for advisers includes honesty, diligence, care, transparency, avoidance of conflicts and compliance with the law.

Example. An adviser acts honestly and in the client's best interest.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The code allows misleading statements about returns.

Grievance redressal

Advisers must have a mechanism to address client complaints, and clients can approach SEBI's complaint system and online dispute resolution if unsatisfied.

Example. A client files a complaint on the regulator's portal after the adviser does not respond.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Clients have no route for complaints against advisers.

Annual audit

Registered advisers must undergo an annual compliance audit and submit reports as required.

Example. A firm gets its compliance reviewed every year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Advisers are never audited.

Prohibition of guaranteed returns

No intermediary may promise or guarantee returns on market-linked products; doing so is a violation.

Example. A representative says, 'You will definitely get 15% every year.'

Exam trap. This statement is false: Intermediaries may guarantee returns if the client is wealthy.
