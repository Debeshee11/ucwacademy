Key terms, examples and exam traps

Derivative

A derivative is a contract whose value depends on an underlying asset, such as a share or an index.

Example. A contract based on the Nifty 50.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A derivative has no link to any underlying.

Futures

A futures contract is an exchange-traded agreement to buy or sell an asset at a fixed price on a future date.

Example. A manager sells index futures to hedge a portfolio.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Futures give a right without an obligation.

Options

An option gives the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell at a fixed price for a premium.

Example. A manager buys a put to protect a position.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An option obliges the buyer to complete the trade.

Hedging

Hedging uses derivatives to offset an existing risk, reducing the portfolio's exposure to adverse moves.

Example. A manager protects holdings with a put option.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Hedging aims to increase risk.

Leverage risk

Derivatives allow large exposure for small margin, magnifying gains and losses.

Example. A small price move leads to a large loss on margin.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Leverage reduces risk.
