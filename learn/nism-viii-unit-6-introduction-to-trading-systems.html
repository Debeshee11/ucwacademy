Key terms, examples and exam traps

Order matching

Orders on the exchange are matched on price-time priority: the best price first, and among equal prices the earliest order.

Example. Two buyers bid the same price and the earlier order is filled first.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Orders are matched on the largest size first.

Order types

Common order types are limit, market, stop loss and IOC (immediate or cancel); each controls price and timing differently.

Example. A trader uses an order that executes immediately or is cancelled.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Only limit orders exist in the derivatives segment.

Market maker

A market maker quotes continuous two-way prices to provide liquidity in a contract.

Example. An entity always quotes both buy and sell prices to keep the market liquid.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A market maker only buys, never sells.

Market-wide position limit

The MWPL caps the total open positions across all traders in a stock's derivatives; when it reaches 95% the stock enters a ban period.

Example. A stock's derivative position exceeds 95% of the limit and is banned for fresh positions.

Exam trap. This statement is false: MWPL applies only to index derivatives.

Ban period

In the ban period, traders can only reduce their open positions in the stock; fresh positions are not allowed.

Example. A trader can square off but cannot build new positions in a stock under restriction.

Exam trap. This statement is false: During a ban period all trading is banned.

Client position limit

Position limits restrict the open positions a single client or member can hold, to prevent concentration and manipulation.

Example. A single client cannot hold unlimited open positions in one stock.

Exam trap. This statement is false: There are no limits for a single client.

Trading hours of derivatives

The equity derivatives segment trades during regular market hours, 9:15 am to 3:30 pm, on business days.

Example. A trader places a futures order at 10 am on a working day.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Equity derivatives trade only in the pre-open session.

Price band in derivatives

Derivatives have operating ranges that stop orders far from the last price, to prevent erroneous trades.

Example. An order far away from the market price is rejected by the system.

Exam trap. This statement is false: There is no price protection in derivatives.

  3. In the ban period, traders can only reduce their open positions in the stock; fresh positions are not allowed.
  4. The daily settlement price of futures is generally the closing price (average of the last half hour), and positions are marked against it.

Q5. Which statement best describes “Order types”?

  1. Common order types are limit, market, stop loss and IOC (immediate or cancel); each controls price and timing differently.
  2. Because only margin is paid upfront, futures offer leverage: a small price change can produce a large percentage gain or loss on margin.
  3. Intrinsic value is the amount by which an option is in the money, and is never negative. For a call it is the greater of (spot − strike) and zero.
  4. A speculator takes positions in derivatives to profit from expected price changes and accepts the risk.
