NISM Series V-A syllabus, unit by unit

The exam covers 12 units. The percentage is the share of questions each unit usually carries, so spend your time in that proportion.

Unit 1 Concept and role of a mutual fund6%

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it in securities under professional management. Unit 1 builds the vocabulary everything else rests on: units, NAV, diversification and why a fund is not a guarantee.

  • Why pooling gives small investors access to diversified portfolios.
  • How NAV changes and what it does not tell you.
  • The difference between a mutual fund and a bank deposit.
  • Types of funds by structure: open-ended, closed-ended, interval.
Unit 2 Fund structure and constituents4%

In India a mutual fund has a three-tier structure of sponsor, trust and asset management company, supported by service providers. This unit teaches who does what, and who checks whom.

  • The role of the sponsor, trustees and AMC.
  • Custodian, registrar and transfer agent duties.
  • Why independence of trustees and directors matters.
Unit 3 Legal and regulatory environment10%

Mutual funds are among the most regulated products in India. This unit covers SEBI, AMFI, the rules distributors must follow, the scheme categories, disclosures and investor grievance routes.

  • SEBI's role and the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations.
  • AMFI, ARN, EUIN and the Code of Conduct.
  • SEBI's scheme categorisation and riskometer.
  • Disclosures, KYC and grievance redressal.
Unit 4 Offer document6%

Before investing, an investor should read the scheme's offer document. This unit teaches the SID, SAI, NFO process and what each contains.

  • SID, SAI and what each contains.
  • NFO rules: price, period, allotment.
  • Where the scheme's risk factors, load and asset allocation are found.
Unit 5 Fund distribution and channel management practices8%

Distributors are the bridge between investors and AMCs. This unit covers channels, plans, commissions, mis-selling and the practices expected of a professional.

  • Direct versus regular plans.
  • Trail commission and how distributors are paid.
  • Mis-selling, churning and suitability.
Unit 6 Accounting, valuation and taxation10%

How is NAV worked out, what costs are charged to investors, and how are gains taxed? This unit mixes formulas with rules and is heavily tested.

  • NAV calculation and valuation of securities.
  • Expense ratio and exit load.
  • Taxation of equity, debt and hybrid funds.
Unit 7 Investor service12%

This is the practical heart of the exam: how transactions work. Cut-off times, applicable NAV, SIP, STP, SWP, nomination, transmission and statements are all in here.

  • Cut-off times and applicable NAV.
  • SIP, STP, SWP and switch.
  • Nomination, joint holders, minors and transmission.
  • Account statements and redemption timelines.
Unit 8 Return, risk and performance of funds10%

Measuring what an investment has earned and at what risk. Unit 8 is where formulas such as CAGR, standard deviation, beta and Sharpe ratio live.

  • Absolute return, CAGR and XIRR.
  • Standard deviation, beta, alpha, Sharpe.
  • Duration, yield to maturity and credit risk in debt funds.
  • Benchmarks and total return indices.
Unit 9 Mutual fund scheme selection10%

How to choose among thousands of schemes. This unit is mostly about SEBI's categories and what each is permitted to hold, plus the criteria a good distributor uses.

  • Equity, debt, hybrid and solution-oriented categories.
  • Market-cap definitions.
  • Criteria for selecting a scheme.
Unit 10 Selecting the right investment products for investors9%

Mutual funds are not the only product. This unit places them beside deposits, bonds, PPF, NPS, gold and insurance, so a distributor can recommend the right tool for the goal.

  • Comparing mutual funds with other products on risk, return, liquidity and tax.
  • Matching products to goals and horizons.
Unit 11 Helping investors with financial planning7%

Financial planning turns a product sale into a client relationship. This unit covers the planning process, goals, life stages, emergency funds and the discipline of reviewing.

  • The financial planning process.
  • Goal-based planning.
  • Life stages and risk capacity.
Unit 12 Recommending model portfolios and financial plans8%

The final unit brings it together: building asset allocations for typical clients, using model portfolios, rebalancing and documenting advice responsibly.

  • Strategic and tactical asset allocation.
  • Model portfolios by risk profile.
  • Rebalancing and documentation.

Q1. Which statement best describes “Risk capacity versus tolerance”?

  1. An emergency fund is money kept in safe and liquid assets, usually three to six months of expenses, for unexpected needs.
  2. A multi cap fund must invest at least 25% each in large, mid and small cap stocks, and at least 75% in equity overall.
  3. A flexi cap fund invests at least 65% in equity with no restriction on market-cap allocation, so the manager can move across large, mid and small cap.
  4. Risk capacity is the ability to bear loss financially, while risk tolerance is willingness to bear it emotionally; advice should respect the lower of the two.

Q2. Which statement best describes “PPF”?

  1. SEBI groups schemes into equity, debt, hybrid, solution oriented and other categories with defined definitions, and each AMC may offer only one scheme per category, with a few exceptions.
  2. Mutual funds disclose NAV daily, portfolios monthly and factsheets regularly, so investors can see where their money is invested.
  3. A sectoral or thematic fund invests at least 80% in a particular sector or theme, such as banking or technology, and carries concentration risk.
  4. The Public Provident Fund is a government-backed savings scheme with a 15-year tenure and tax-free interest, with limited liquidity.

Q3. Which term is described here? Mutual funds publish portfolios of all schemes monthly on their websites and with AMFI, and publish the half-yearly financial results.

  1. Portfolio disclosure
  2. Multi cap fund
  3. Economies of scale
  4. Riskometer

Q4. Which term is described here? A model portfolio is a pre-defined allocation for a risk profile, used as a template and customised for the client.

  1. Model portfolio
  2. Financial plan
  3. PPF
  4. Glide path
