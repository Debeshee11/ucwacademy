Key terms, examples and exam traps

Investing versus speculation

Investing commits capital for an expected return after analysis of value, while speculation takes a short-term bet mainly on price movement.

Example. A manager buys a company after valuing its cash flows.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Speculation and investing differ only in the amount of money.

Return objective

A return objective states the return a portfolio needs, taking in the client's goals, inflation and the risk the client can accept.

Example. A client needs 12% a year to fund a goal.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The return objective is fixed without reference to the client.

Risk tolerance and ability

Risk tolerance is the client's willingness to take risk, while ability is the financial capacity to bear loss; the lower of the two governs.

Example. A willing client with little spare capital gets a cautious portfolio.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Willingness alone decides how much risk to take.

Investment constraints

Constraints such as liquidity, time horizon, tax, legal limits and unique needs restrict how a portfolio can be built.

Example. A client who needs cash in two years cannot hold illiquid assets.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Constraints do not affect asset selection.

Primary market

In the primary market, securities are issued for the first time and the issuer receives the proceeds.

Example. A company raises capital through an IPO.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Companies receive the money when shares trade between investors.

Secondary market

In the secondary market, existing securities are traded between investors, giving liquidity and price discovery.

Example. A fund sells shares on the exchange.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The issuer receives funds in every secondary trade.

Clearing and settlement

A clearing corporation guarantees settlement of trades and manages counterparty risk, with delivery of securities and funds through depositories and banks.

Example. A trade is settled through the clearing corporation.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Settlement risk is borne wholly by the buyer.

Market intermediaries

Brokers, depositories, custodians, registrars and portfolio managers each perform a distinct role in the securities chain.

Example. A custodian safeguards client securities of a portfolio manager.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A portfolio manager also acts as the depository.

Q2. Which statement best describes “Return objective”?

  1. Clients have rights under the investor charter and can seek redress from the manager, then SEBI's complaint platform and online dispute resolution.
  2. A return objective states the return a portfolio needs, taking in the client's goals, inflation and the risk the client can accept.
  3. CAPM says expected return equals the risk-free rate plus beta times the market risk premium.
  4. A portfolio manager must be registered with SEBI and must meet the eligibility, capital and fit and proper requirements.

Q3. Which statement best describes “Secondary market”?

  1. Stress testing evaluates portfolio losses under extreme but plausible scenarios, complementing statistical measures.
  2. In the secondary market, existing securities are traded between investors, giving liquidity and price discovery.
  3. A portfolio manager is a person who, under a contract with a client, advises, directs or manages the client's securities or funds.
  4. A mutual fund is a trust that pools investor money, run by an AMC, with a trustee and a custodian providing oversight and safekeeping.

Q4. Which statement best describes “Investing versus speculation”?

  1. Investing commits capital for an expected return after analysis of value, while speculation takes a short-term bet mainly on price movement.

  3. The disclosure document gives clients the manager's investment approach, fees, risks, performance and other facts, and is filed with SEBI.
  4. YTM is the single discount rate that equates the bond's price to the present value of its cash flows if held to maturity.

  4. In the primary market, securities are issued for the first time and the issuer receives the proceeds.
