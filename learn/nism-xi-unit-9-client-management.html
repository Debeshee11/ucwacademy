Key terms, examples and exam traps

Client onboarding

Onboarding includes KYC, risk profiling, signing documents and explaining services, fees and risks.

Example. A new client completes forms and receives a welcome kit.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Onboarding is complete when the first order is placed.

Risk profiling

Understanding the client's income, experience and tolerance helps in providing suitable products and avoiding mis-selling.

Example. A dealer asks about a client's experience before enabling derivatives.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Risk profiling is optional.

Suitability

A dealer should recommend only products and trades suitable to the client's profile and avoid pushing high-risk trades to inexperienced clients.

Example. A dealer advises a first-time investor on the risks of leverage.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A dealer should encourage maximum trading to earn brokerage.

Disclosures

Brokers must disclose brokerage, charges, risks, and the grievance process clearly to clients.

Example. A dealer tells the client the full list of charges.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Brokers may hide charges.

Unauthorised trading

Trading in a client's account without her instructions is prohibited and exposes the broker to penalties.

Example. A dealer places trades without asking the client.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Dealers may trade freely in an account with consent given once for all.

Churning

Churning is excessive trading in a client's account to generate brokerage rather than serving the client's needs.

Example. A dealer pushes a client to trade repeatedly for commission.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Churning benefits the client.

Confidentiality

Client information must be kept confidential and shared only with consent or as legally required.

Example. A dealer does not share a client's holdings with others.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A dealer may share client data freely.

Complaint handling

Complaints must be recorded, acknowledged and resolved within prescribed timelines, with escalation to the exchange or SEBI if unresolved.

Example. A firm logs a complaint and responds within the time limit.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Complaints can be ignored if the client is wrong.

Dormant accounts

Accounts with no activity for a defined period are marked dormant and need re-verification before use.

Example. A client who has not traded for years must update KYC.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Dormant accounts are closed automatically.

Honest communication

Staff must not make false statements, promise returns or give tips as guaranteed outcomes.

Example. A dealer says there is no risk in a trade.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A dealer may assure profits to build trust.

Conflict of interest

Staff must avoid conflicts, such as trading ahead of clients, and disclose any that cannot be avoided.

Example. A dealer discloses that he holds the stock he recommends.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Conflicts are not relevant in broking.

Record keeping and statements

Brokers must provide periodic statements of accounts and holdings and maintain records for the specified period.

Example. A client receives monthly statements of funds and securities.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Brokers need not give statements.
