Key terms, examples and exam traps

Asset class

An asset class is a group of investments with similar characteristics, such as equity, fixed income, gold and real estate.

Example. An investor splits money between shares, bonds and gold.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Equity and debt are the same asset class.

Risk and return

Higher expected return generally requires acceptance of higher risk; investors are paid for bearing risk.

Example. A client chasing higher returns must accept larger swings.

Exam trap. This statement is false: High return can be earned without any added risk.

Time value of money

Money received sooner is worth more than the same sum later because it can earn a return.

Example. ₹1 crore today is worth more than ₹1 crore in five years.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The time value of money is the interest on a loan.

Compounding

Compounding generates returns on past returns, growing wealth faster over long horizons.

Example. Reinvested gains themselves earn further gains.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Compounding means returns on the initial sum only.

Inflation

Inflation reduces the purchasing power of money; real return is nominal return minus inflation, approximately.

Example. A 10% return with 6% inflation yields about 4% real return.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Inflation raises the real return of cash.

Risk types

Investment risks include market, credit, liquidity, interest-rate and concentration risks.

Example. A concentrated portfolio suffers when its top holding falls.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Concentration risk applies only to bonds.

Risk profile

A client's risk profile combines willingness and ability to bear loss and guides the portfolio mix.

Example. A client with a short horizon and low tolerance has a conservative profile.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Risk profile is irrelevant to PMS.

Diversification

Diversification spreads investments across securities and asset classes to reduce specific risk.

Example. A portfolio holding 25 stocks across sectors.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Diversification removes all risk.

Investment horizon

The investment horizon is the period for which funds can remain invested, affecting what assets are suitable.

Example. A client who needs funds in 6 months has a short horizon.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Horizon has no effect on asset choice.
