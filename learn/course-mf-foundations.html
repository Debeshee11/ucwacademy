Key ideas you will take away

  • AUM = money flows + market movement. It is not a measure of returns.
  • Record SIPs in a falling month signal disciplined investors, not panic.
  • An SIP is a method, not a product — returns come from the fund.
  • Use XIRR for SIPs; don't compare with point-to-point returns.
  • Category inflows measure sentiment, not future returns.
  • The behaviour gap is usually bigger than the cost gap.

Try a question

Q1. Industry AUM has grown many times over the last decade. A client concludes: "If industry AUM is growing this fast, mutual fund investors must be earning excellent returns." What is the most important distinction to explain?

  1. Rising AUM always indicates superior fund performance.
  2. Industry AUM grows through both market appreciation and net inflows, so industry growth alone says nothing about the returns a particular investor earned.
  3. Industry AUM is driven entirely by SIP contributions.
  4. Growing AUM means mutual funds are becoming less risky.
Show the answer

B. AUM is the combined result of money coming in and market movements on money already invested. A year of heavy inflows can push AUM up even if many investors who joined that year are sitting on losses. Returns are an investor-level outcome: which fund, when they invested, how long they stayed.

Q2. The client above asks whether starting his SIP was a mistake. Which response shows the best understanding?

  1. "SIPs never lose money over the long run, so this is temporary."
  2. "An SIP spreads your buying across market levels, but it can't stop losses when the market falls. Most of your instalments were invested at higher prices, so you're showing a loss today. Your goal is in 2034, and the instalments you're buying now are at lower prices. Let's check the fund still suits that goal rather than judge it on two years."
  3. "You should switch to a fund that has performed better in the last year."
  4. "Stop the SIP until markets recover, then restart."
Show the answer

B. It is honest about what SIPs can and cannot do, explains why the return is negative in plain language, links back to his 2034 goal, and suggests a sensible review instead of a reaction.
