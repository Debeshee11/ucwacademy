Key terms, examples and exam traps

GDP

Gross Domestic Product is the total value of goods and services produced within a country in a period, a measure of economic activity.

Example. A country's total output for the year rises 6.5%.

Exam trap. This statement is false: GDP is the total government spending.

Inflation

Inflation is the sustained rise in the general price level, measured in India by the Consumer Price Index and the Wholesale Price Index.

Example. The general price level rises 5% compared with last year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Inflation is a fall in the general price level.

CPI and WPI

CPI measures changes in retail prices faced by consumers, while WPI measures wholesale prices of goods. The RBI targets CPI inflation.

Example. The policy target is based on retail prices.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The RBI targets WPI inflation.

Fiscal deficit

The fiscal deficit is government expenditure minus revenue (excluding borrowings), showing how much the government must borrow.

Example. The government spends ₹50 lakh crore against receipts of ₹45 lakh crore.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The fiscal deficit equals the trade deficit.

Current account deficit

The current account deficit arises when a country's imports of goods, services and transfers exceed its exports.

Example. India buys more from the world than it sells.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A current account deficit means the government has a budget deficit.

Monetary policy

Monetary policy is the central bank's use of interest rates and liquidity tools to manage inflation and growth.

Example. The RBI raises the repo rate to control prices.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Monetary policy is set by the finance ministry.

Fiscal policy

Fiscal policy is the government's use of taxation and spending to influence the economy.

Example. The government cuts taxes to stimulate demand.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Fiscal policy is set by the central bank.

Business cycle

The business cycle consists of expansion, peak, contraction and trough, with different sectors outperforming in each phase.

Example. An economy moves from recovery to boom and then slowdown.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The economy grows in a straight line without cycles.

Leading indicators

Leading indicators such as new orders and PMI change before the economy turns and help forecast direction.

Example. A rise in new orders signals growth ahead.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Leading indicators change after the economy turns.

PMI

The Purchasing Managers' Index surveys business activity; a reading above 50 indicates expansion and below 50 contraction.

Example. A manufacturing PMI of 56 shows expansion.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A PMI below 50 shows expansion.

IIP

The Index of Industrial Production tracks changes in the volume of production in mining, manufacturing and electricity.

Example. A monthly number shows industrial output rising 4%.

Exam trap. This statement is false: IIP measures retail inflation.

Exchange rate impact

A weaker rupee helps exporters' revenues but raises import costs, affecting sectors differently.

Example. IT exporters gain when the rupee depreciates.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A weaker rupee hurts all companies equally.

Interest rate impact

Higher interest rates raise borrowing costs and discount rates, which usually pressure valuations and rate-sensitive sectors.

Example. A rate hike hurts real estate and auto stocks.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Higher interest rates always lift all share prices.
