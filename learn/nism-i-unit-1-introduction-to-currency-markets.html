Key terms, examples and exam traps

Exchange rate

An exchange rate is the price of one currency in terms of another.

Example. USD-INR at 83 means one dollar costs 83 rupees.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An exchange rate is the interest rate on currency loans.

Base and quote currency

In a pair quoted as A-B, A is the base currency and B is the quote currency, and the rate shows units of B per unit of A.

Example. In USD-INR, the dollar is the base and the rupee is quoted.

Exam trap. This statement is false: In USD-INR, the rupee is the base currency.

Direct and indirect quote

A direct quote gives the home currency per unit of foreign currency; an indirect quote gives foreign currency per unit of home currency.

Example. For an Indian dealer, 83 rupees per dollar is direct.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A direct quote shows foreign currency per rupee.

Bid and ask

The bid is the price at which a dealer buys the base currency and the ask is the price at which the dealer sells it, with the ask higher than the bid.

Example. A dealer quotes 82.98-83.02.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The bid is higher than the ask.

Spread

The spread is the difference between ask and bid and is the dealer's margin and a measure of liquidity.

Example. A narrow spread shows a liquid pair.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A wide spread signals high liquidity.

Appreciation and depreciation

A currency appreciates when it buys more foreign currency and depreciates when it buys less.

Example. USD-INR rising from 82 to 84 means the rupee has depreciated.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A rising USD-INR means the rupee has strengthened.

Cross rate

A cross rate between two currencies is derived from their rates against a third, usually the dollar.

Example. EUR-INR is computed from EUR-USD and USD-INR.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Cross rates are fixed by the central bank.

Spot market

In the spot market currencies are bought and sold for settlement in two working days.

Example. A bank buys dollars for delivery after two days.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Spot trades are settled after one year.

Interbank market

The interbank market is where banks trade large amounts of currency among themselves and sets reference rates.

Example. Banks quote to each other over electronic platforms.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The interbank market is only for retail customers.

Market participants

Participants include banks, corporates, exporters, importers, investors, speculators and central banks.

Example. An importer buys dollars to pay a foreign supplier.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Only banks take part in currency markets.

Hedgers and speculators

Hedgers use derivatives to reduce existing currency risk; speculators take risk hoping to profit from price moves.

Example. An exporter sells dollars forward to lock in a rate.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Hedgers aim to profit from price moves.

Arbitrageurs

Arbitrageurs exploit price differences in the same or related instruments across markets to earn low-risk profit.

Example. A trader buys dollars on one venue and sells on another at a higher price.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Arbitrageurs take large directional bets.

Exchange rate regimes

Regimes range from fixed to floating; India follows a managed float where the central bank may intervene to curb volatility.

Example. The RBI sells dollars to steady the rupee.

Exam trap. This statement is false: India has a rigidly fixed rate.

Drivers of exchange rates

Rates move with interest rate differentials, inflation, trade and capital flows, growth and sentiment.

Example. Heavy foreign portfolio inflows strengthen the rupee.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Exchange rates depend only on exports.

Purchasing power parity

PPP says exchange rates adjust so that price levels are equal across countries, so higher inflation tends to weaken a currency.

Example. A country with high inflation sees its currency weaken over time.

Exam trap. This statement is false: PPP says high inflation strengthens the currency.

Foreign exchange reserves

Reserves are foreign currency assets held by the central bank to manage the exchange rate and meet external obligations.

Example. The RBI uses reserves to curb sharp falls.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Reserves are held by commercial banks only.

Balance of payments

The balance of payments records a country's transactions with the rest of the world and is an important driver of currency demand.

Example. A large current account deficit puts pressure on the currency.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The balance of payments records only government spending.
