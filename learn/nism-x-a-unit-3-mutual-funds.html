Key terms, examples and exam traps

Mutual fund structure

A mutual fund has a sponsor, trust, trustees and an AMC, supported by a custodian and RTA, and is regulated by SEBI.

Example. A three-tier structure oversees the investor's money.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A mutual fund is a bank deposit.

Direct plan for advisers

A registered investment adviser charging a fee should generally recommend direct plans to avoid receiving commissions.

Example. An adviser who charges a fee puts the client into the lower-cost plan.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An adviser charging a fee can also collect trail commission from the same client.

NAV

NAV is the per-unit value of a scheme, equal to total assets less liabilities divided by units outstanding.

Example. A scheme with ₹100 crore net assets and 5 crore units has a NAV of ₹20.

Exam trap. This statement is false: NAV is the price paid for shares on the exchange.

Equity fund categories

SEBI defines categories such as large cap, mid cap, small cap, flexi cap and ELSS, each with minimum equity allocations.

Example. A scheme must keep at least 80% in top-100 stocks to be called a large cap fund.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A large cap fund can hold any mix of stocks.

Debt fund risk

Debt funds are exposed to interest-rate risk, credit risk and liquidity risk, and returns are not guaranteed.

Example. A bond fund loses value when interest rates rise.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Debt funds are free of risk.

Hybrid funds

Hybrid funds combine equity and debt, with varied allocations, to balance growth and stability.

Example. A client wanting moderate risk holds a scheme with 65% equity and 35% debt.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Hybrid funds invest only in debt.

Expense ratio

The expense ratio is the annual charge as a percentage of a scheme's assets and reduces the investor's return.

Example. A scheme charging 1.5% costs the investor ₹1,500 yearly on ₹1 lakh.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The expense ratio is charged only at entry.

Exit load

Exit load is charged on redemption within a defined period and is credited to the scheme.

Example. A fund levies 1% if units are sold within a year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Exit load is a commission paid to the adviser.

SIP

A Systematic Investment Plan invests a fixed sum at regular intervals and helps to average the purchase cost over time.

Example. A client invests ₹10,000 monthly in a fund.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A SIP assures positive returns.

Riskometer

The riskometer shows a scheme's risk on six levels from low to very high, and should match the client's risk profile.

Example. An adviser compares the scheme's risk label with the client's tolerance.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Riskometer shows the scheme's past return.

Tax on mutual funds

Equity-oriented funds are taxed on short- and long-term gains at different rates, while specified debt funds are taxed at the investor's slab rate.

Example. A debt fund gain is added to the investor's total income.

Exam trap. This statement is false: All mutual fund gains are tax-free.

Fund selection

An adviser compares funds on risk-adjusted return, consistency, costs, portfolio quality and fit with the client's goals, not just recent performance.

Example. An adviser reviews five-year rolling returns, not just last year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The previous year's best fund is always the right choice.

ETF and index funds

ETFs and index funds replicate an index at low cost; ETFs trade on exchanges while index funds are bought from the AMC.

Example. A client buys a low-cost fund that mirrors the Nifty 50.

Exam trap. This statement is false: ETFs are bought only through the AMC at NAV.

Portfolio overlap

Holding several funds with similar portfolios increases overlap and reduces diversification benefits.

Example. A client owns five large-cap funds with the same top holdings.

Exam trap. This statement is false: More funds always mean better diversification.
