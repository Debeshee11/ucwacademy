NISM Series V-D syllabus, unit by unit

The exam covers 12 units. The percentage is the share of questions each unit usually carries, so spend your time in that proportion.

Unit 1 Investment landscape, mutual fund concept and structure8%

Asset classes, risk and return, how a mutual fund pools money and the sponsor-trustee-AMC structure.

  • Risk and return.
  • Role of a mutual fund.
  • Sponsor, trust, AMC, custodian.
Unit 2 Legal, regulatory framework and scheme information9%

SEBI regulation, the role of AMFI, scheme documents, offer rules and scheme categories.

  • SEBI Mutual Fund Regulations.
  • SID, SAI and KIM.
  • Scheme categorisation.
Unit 3 Distribution, NAV, expenses and tax8%

How funds are sold, how NAV and TER work and how mutual fund income is taxed.

  • Distribution channels.
  • NAV and TER.
  • Taxation of gains.
Unit 4 Investor services, risk, performance and scheme selection12%

Account services, KYC, risk profiling, performance measures and choosing a scheme.

  • KYC and services.
  • Risk measures.
  • Return measures.
  • Suitability.
Unit 5 Specialized Investment Funds: framework and rules8%

What a SIF is, how it sits between mutual funds and PMS, the strategies, the ₹10 lakh threshold, limits and distributor duties.

  • Minimum investment.
  • Strategy rules.
  • Limits and redemption.
  • Suitability for SIFs.
Unit 6 Basics of derivatives and understanding index10%

What derivatives are, who uses them, the types of contracts and how stock indices are built.

  • Hedgers, speculators, arbitrageurs.
  • Forwards, futures, options, swaps.
  • Index construction.
Unit 7 Forwards and futures10%

Futures contracts, pricing, margins, mark-to-market and settlement.

  • Forward vs future.
  • Cost of carry.
  • Margins and MTM.
  • Settlement.
Unit 8 Options9%

Calls, puts, premium, moneyness, payoffs, pricing drivers and Greeks.

  • Call and put.
  • Intrinsic and time value.
  • Moneyness.
  • Greeks.
Unit 9 Strategies using equity futures and options6%

Hedging, spreads, straddles and how funds and SIFs use equity derivatives.

  • Hedges.
  • Covered call and protective put.
  • Straddle and spreads.
Unit 10 Interest rates, fixed income markets and interest rate derivatives5%

Interest rate basics, bond price and yield, and why interest rate derivatives exist.

  • Bond price and yield.
  • Duration.
  • Why IRDs.
Unit 11 Interest rate futures and options11%

How exchange-traded interest rate futures and options are built, priced and settled.

  • Notional bond and T-bill futures.
  • PV01 and hedge ratio.
  • Interest rate options.
Unit 12 Strategies using interest rate derivatives and distributor conduct4%

Hedging and trading strategies with interest rate derivatives and the distributor's duties for complex products.

  • Hedge a bond portfolio.
  • Adjust duration.
  • Ethics and disclosure.

  1. Bond price and yield
  2. Role of trustees
  3. Grievance redressal
  4. Open-ended and closed-ended

Q3. Which statement best describes “Derivative risk”?

  1. Standard deviation measures how much returns vary around their average, so a higher value means a more volatile fund.
  2. A distributor needs an AMFI Registration Number to sell funds, and an EUIN identifies the employee or sales person who gave advice.
  3. A clearing corporation becomes the counterparty to both sides of every exchange-traded contract, reducing counterparty risk.
  4. Leverage in derivatives magnifies both gains and losses, so position limits and margins matter.

Q4. Which statement best describes “AMFI”?

  1. AMFI is the industry body of AMCs that promotes standards, sets code of conduct for distributors and registers them with an ARN.
  2. Put-call parity links call, put, spot and the present value of strike for European options with the same strike and expiry.
  3. If market movement reduces an investor's holding below ₹10 lakh, the investor can only redeem the entire remaining amount rather than part.
  4. Stamp duty is charged at 0.005% on purchase of units, including switches and SIP instalments.
