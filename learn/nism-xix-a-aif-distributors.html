Q1. Which statement best describes “Direct plan”?

  1. AIFs must provide an option for investors to invest directly, without a distributor, and distribution commissions should be transparent.
  2. Category II AIFs, such as private equity and debt funds, do not undertake leverage except for day-to-day operational needs and are not given specific incentives.
  3. Distributors of AIFs must hold the NISM XIX-A certification and follow the code of conduct, including disclosure and suitability.
  4. Funds set up in GIFT City's IFSC can pool money in foreign currency and are governed by a separate regulatory framework.

Q2. Which statement best describes “Conflict of interest policy”?

  1. An AIF is a privately pooled investment vehicle that raises money from investors for investing as per a defined policy, regulated by SEBI.
  2. The manager must have a policy to identify, disclose and manage conflicts, such as allocating deals between funds.
  3. A commitment is the total amount an investor agrees to invest in the fund, to be called in instalments over time.
  4. Category I and II AIFs enjoy pass-through status: income other than business income is taxed in the investors' hands, retaining its character.

Q3. Which term is described here? An MFN clause lets an investor elect the better terms granted to other investors in comparable side letters.

  1. Strategy fit
  2. Minimum investment
  3. Most-favoured-nation clause
  4. Ongoing manager review

Q4. Which statement best describes “Alignment of interest”?

  1. Alignment is shown by meaningful manager commitment, performance-linked pay and fees that reward results rather than assets alone.
  2. Reviewing the fund's concentration by company, sector and vintage reveals risks of overexposure.
  3. Screening filters opportunities against the fund's strategy, size, sector and risk criteria before detailed work begins.
  4. Investors, especially large ones, negotiate on fees, governance rights, reporting, key-person triggers and transfer terms.
