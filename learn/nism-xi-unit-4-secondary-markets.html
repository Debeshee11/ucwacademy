Key terms, examples and exam traps

Trading session

Regular equity trading takes place from 9:15 am to 3:30 pm, preceded by a pre-open session from 9:00 am to 9:15 am.

Example. An investor places an order at 11 am on a working day.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Equity trading runs all night.

Pre-open session

The pre-open session uses a call auction between 9:00 and 9:15 am to determine opening prices.

Example. An order is placed before the market opens to discover the opening price.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The pre-open session executes trades continuously.

Order matching

Orders are matched by price-time priority: best price first and, at the same price, earliest order first.

Example. An earlier bid at the same price is filled first.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Orders are matched by size alone.

Tick size

The tick size is the minimum price movement, generally ₹0.05 for equity shares.

Example. A price can change in steps of five paise.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Tick size is the number of shares in a lot.

Market lot

In the equity cash segment the market lot is one share, so investors can trade any quantity.

Example. An investor buys 7 shares of a company.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The cash segment requires a lot of 100 shares.

Trading segments

Segments include normal, trade-for-trade and surveillance-restricted ones, each with different conditions on trading.

Example. A stock in a restricted segment cannot be squared off intraday.

Exam trap. This statement is false: All stocks trade under the same conditions.

Types of trades

Trades are classified as delivery-based or intraday (squared off within the day), with different charges and margins.

Example. A client buys and sells within the day without taking delivery.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Intraday trades require delivery at the end of the day.
