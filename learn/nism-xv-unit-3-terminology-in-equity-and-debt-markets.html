Key terms, examples and exam traps

Market capitalisation

Market capitalisation is share price multiplied by shares outstanding, and classifies companies as large, mid or small cap.

Example. A company with 50 crore shares at ₹200 has a value of ₹10,000 crore.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Market capitalisation is the company's total assets.

Yield to maturity

Yield to maturity is the annualised return on a bond if held to maturity and coupons are reinvested at the same rate.

Example. A bond priced below face value has a yield above its coupon.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Yield to maturity equals the coupon rate always.

Face value and issue price

Face value is the nominal value of a security, while the issue price is what investors pay at the time of issue, which may differ.

Example. A share with face value ₹10 is issued at ₹250.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Issue price is always equal to face value.

Credit spread

Credit spread is the extra yield on a risky bond over a risk-free government bond of similar maturity, compensating for credit risk.

Example. A corporate bond yields 2% more than a similar G-sec.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Credit spread is the difference between bid and ask.
