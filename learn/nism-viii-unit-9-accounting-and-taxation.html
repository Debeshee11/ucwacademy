Key terms, examples and exam traps

Non-speculative business income

Profits or losses from index and stock futures and options are generally treated as non-speculative business income under income tax law.

Example. A trader reports F&O profits as business income, not capital gains.

Exam trap. This statement is false: F&O gains are always taxed as capital gains.

Securities transaction tax on derivatives

STT is charged on derivatives transactions, for example on the sale of options on the premium and on futures sale, at rates set by law.

Example. A small tax is deducted on the sell side of a futures trade.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Derivatives are exempt from all transaction taxes.

Tax audit

A person whose derivatives turnover or losses cross specified thresholds may need to get accounts audited under the tax law.

Example. A trader with large F&O turnover must have accounts audited.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Tax audit is never required for traders.

Mark-to-market accounting

Open futures positions are marked to market at year end for accounting, and unrealised losses may be recognised.

Example. Open positions are valued at the closing price on the balance sheet date.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Open positions are ignored at year end.

Carry forward of business losses

Non-speculative business losses from derivatives can be set off against other business income and carried forward for eight years if returns are filed on time.

Example. A trader's F&O loss is carried forward to future years.

Exam trap. This statement is false: F&O losses can never be carried forward.

Turnover for derivatives

For tax audit purposes, turnover of futures and options is computed using specified methods, such as the absolute profit and loss and premium on sale of options.

Example. Turnover is computed by adding absolute profits and losses for futures.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Turnover for F&O is equal to the contract value.
