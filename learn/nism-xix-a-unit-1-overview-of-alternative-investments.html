Key terms, examples and exam traps

Alternative investments

Alternative investments are assets other than listed equity, bonds and cash, such as private equity, venture capital, hedge funds, real estate and infrastructure.

Example. An investor puts money in an unlisted growth company through a fund.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Alternative investments are the same as listed shares.

Private equity

Private equity invests in unlisted companies, usually taking significant ownership, with the aim of growing value and exiting later.

Example. A fund buys a stake in a private manufacturer to expand it.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Private equity invests only in listed companies.

Venture capital

Venture capital funds early-stage, high-growth companies, accepting high failure rates for the chance of outsized returns.

Example. A fund backs a start-up with a new technology.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Venture capital funds only mature, stable firms.

Hedge fund strategies

Hedge funds use strategies such as long-short, market-neutral, event-driven and macro, often with leverage and derivatives.

Example. A fund buys undervalued shares and sells overvalued ones short.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Hedge funds can only buy and hold equities.

Illiquidity

Alternatives are typically illiquid, with money locked for years and limited exit options before the end of the fund's life.

Example. An investor cannot withdraw until the fund winds up.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Alternatives offer daily redemption.

Low correlation

Many alternatives show low correlation with listed markets, offering diversification, though valuations are less frequent.

Example. A real-estate fund does not move with the stock market.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Alternatives always move exactly with equities.

Manager skill

Returns from alternatives depend heavily on manager skill, so dispersion between top and bottom managers is wide.

Example. The gap between the best and worst PE fund is large.

Exam trap. This statement is false: All alternative managers deliver similar returns.
