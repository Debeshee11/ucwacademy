Key terms, examples and exam traps

Pledge

A pledge is the delivery of securities as security for a loan, with the pledged securities blocked in the borrower's account.

Example. A borrower pledges shares to a bank for a loan.

Exam trap. This statement is false: In a pledge the lender never holds any claim on the securities.

Pledgor and pledgee

The pledgor is the borrower who pledges the securities and the pledgee is the lender who accepts them as security.

Example. The bank is the pledgee and the borrower is the pledgor.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The pledgee is the owner who offers the securities.

Pledge creation and confirmation

The pledgor requests the pledge through her DP and the pledgee confirms it, so the pledge is created only after confirmation.

Example. A pledge becomes effective after the lender confirms it.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A pledge is complete when the pledgor submits the request.

Pledge closure

When the loan is repaid, the pledgee closes the pledge, or both parties request closure, and the securities are released.

Example. After repayment, the bank closes the pledge.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The pledgor can close a pledge alone without the pledgee's consent.

Invocation

If the pledgor defaults, the pledgee can invoke the pledge, and securities are moved to the pledgee's account.

Example. The lender invokes the pledge after repeated default.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The pledgor invokes a pledge when she wants her shares back.

Rights of the pledgor

While securities are pledged, the pledgor continues to enjoy corporate benefits such as dividends, as the pledgor remains the beneficial owner.

Example. A borrower still receives a dividend on pledged shares.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The pledgee receives all corporate benefits throughout the pledge.

Hypothecation

In a hypothecation, the securities remain in the borrower's account but are blocked in favour of the lender, who gets a charge without possession.

Example. A borrower hypothecates shares to a bank, which holds a charge on them.

Exam trap. This statement is false: In hypothecation the lender takes the securities into its own account.

Margin pledge

Clients give securities as margin to brokers by creating a margin pledge in favour of the clearing member, and the clearing member may re-pledge them to the clearing corporation.

Example. A client pledges shares as margin for derivatives positions.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Clients must transfer shares to the broker's own account to give margin.

Restrictions on pledged securities

Pledged securities cannot be sold or transferred until the pledge is closed or invoked, and any partial release is handled by the pledgee.

Example. A client must first release the pledge before selling the shares.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Pledged shares can be sold freely by the pledgor.
