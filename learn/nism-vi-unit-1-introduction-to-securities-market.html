Key terms, examples and exam traps

Securities

Securities include shares, debentures, government securities, units of mutual funds and similar instruments that can be issued and traded.

Example. A share of a listed company and a government bond are both securities.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Only shares are securities.

Primary market

The primary market is where new securities are issued by companies to raise funds directly from investors.

Example. A company launches an initial public offer to raise capital.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The primary market is where existing shares are traded among investors.

Secondary market

The secondary market is where already-issued securities are bought and sold between investors, on stock exchanges.

Example. An investor sells her listed shares through a broker on an exchange.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The secondary market raises fresh capital for the issuer.

Stock exchange

A stock exchange provides a platform on which members trade securities under rules and surveillance.

Example. Trades in listed shares are executed on a recognised exchange.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A stock exchange issues shares to the public.

Clearing corporation

A clearing corporation guarantees and settles trades made on an exchange, managing the obligations of members.

Example. The clearing corporation works out what each broker must pay or deliver.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The clearing corporation holds investors' securities in demat form.

Rolling settlement

In rolling settlement every trade is settled a fixed number of working days after the trading day; equity trades settle on a T+1 basis.

Example. A share bought on Monday is settled on the next working day.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A weekly account period settles all trades together on Friday.

Role of SEBI

SEBI regulates the securities market, protects investors and promotes market development.

Example. SEBI frames the rules that depositories and DPs must follow.

Exam trap. This statement is false: SEBI trades securities on behalf of investors.

Market intermediaries

Intermediaries such as brokers, depository participants, merchant bankers and registrars help investors use the market.

Example. An investor needs a broker to trade and a DP to hold shares.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Investors can trade on an exchange without any intermediary.
