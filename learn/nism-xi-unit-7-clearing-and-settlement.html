Key terms, examples and exam traps

Rolling settlement

In rolling settlement, trades done each day are settled after a fixed number of days, now T+1 for equity.

Example. A trade on Monday is settled on Tuesday.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Equity settlement takes place weekly.

Pay-in and pay-out

Pay-in is the delivery of securities and funds to the clearing corporation, and pay-out is the release to the buyer and seller.

Example. Sellers deliver shares and buyers pay money, and then each receives what is due.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Pay-out is the payment to the clearing corporation.

Clearing member

A clearing member settles trades with the clearing corporation for itself or others.

Example. An entity that deals with the clearing corporation for trades.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A clearing member advises clients on shares.

Novation

Novation substitutes the clearing corporation as counterparty to each trade, guaranteeing settlement.

Example. After the trade, the buyer and seller each deal with the clearing corporation.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Novation ends the trade.

Settlement guarantee fund

The settlement guarantee fund is a pool maintained by the clearing corporation to complete settlement when a member defaults.

Example. A fund exists to complete settlements if a broker fails.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The fund pays profits to clients.

Delivery instruction

A client instructs the depository participant to transfer shares for settlement using a delivery instruction slip or electronic authorisation.

Example. A seller authorises transfer of shares to settle her sale.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Delivery instructions are used to place orders.

Short delivery

If the seller fails to deliver, the shortage is settled through an auction or closed out at a set price.

Example. The seller does not deliver, and the exchange auctions the shares.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A short delivery is cancelled with no consequences.

Close-out

Close-out is the compulsory settlement of a position that cannot be delivered, at a price determined by the exchange.

Example. A seller who cannot deliver sees the trade closed out at a specified price.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Close-out is the buyer's penalty.

Funds settlement

Funds are settled through clearing banks, with the clearing corporation debiting and crediting member accounts.

Example. The exchange moves funds between members' bank accounts.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Funds are settled only in cash at the broker's office.

Settlement calendar

The exchange publishes a settlement calendar, including holidays, to determine pay-in and pay-out dates.

Example. A trade near a holiday settles after the holiday.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Settlement dates are the same irrespective of holidays.

Corporate action adjustments

Settlement of trades around corporate actions follows the ex-date, with the buyer entitled only if bought before the ex-date.

Example. A buyer on the ex-date does not get the dividend.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A buyer on the ex-date is entitled to the dividend.

Settlement default

If a member defaults, the clearing corporation uses the member's deposits and other resources to complete obligations.

Example. The member's margin is used to settle.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The clients of the defaulting member always lose everything.
