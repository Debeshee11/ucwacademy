Key terms, examples and exam traps

Know your client

Intermediaries must know the client's identity, financial position and investment objectives before allowing derivative trades.

Example. A broker collects income and experience details from a new client.

Exam trap. This statement is false: KYC is needed only for futures and not for options.

Code of conduct

A code of conduct for brokers requires honesty, fairness, due diligence and prohibits misleading statements and unfair practices.

Example. A broker must not promise assured profits.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Brokers can guarantee returns in derivatives.

Conflict of interest

A dealer must avoid or disclose conflicts, such as trading ahead of client orders (front running), which is prohibited.

Example. A dealer who trades for his own account before executing a large client order commits a violation.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Front running is permitted if the client is not aware.

Complaint handling

Intermediaries must have a grievance mechanism and resolve client complaints promptly, and escalate to the exchange or SEBI if not resolved.

Example. A client with an unresolved complaint writes to the exchange.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Clients can complain only after a year.

Appropriate advice

Dealers should explain leverage, margin calls and potential loss and avoid pushing high-risk strategies to unsuitable clients.

Example. A dealer explains that a short option can lose more than the premium collected.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Dealers should hide risk to encourage trades.

  1. The tick size is the minimum price movement allowed for a contract, such as ₹0.05 for futures on equity.
  2. On the expiry date the futures price converges to the spot price of the underlying.
  3. Margin can be given in cash, bank guarantees, fixed deposit receipts or approved securities, subject to haircuts.
  4. A dealer must avoid or disclose conflicts, such as trading ahead of client orders (front running), which is prohibited.

Q2. Which statement best describes “Know your client”?

  1. A clearing member is authorised to clear and settle trades with the clearing corporation, for itself or for trading members.
  2. Intermediaries must know the client's identity, financial position and investment objectives before allowing derivative trades.
  3. The call seller (writer) receives the premium and must sell the underlying at the strike if exercised. The maximum profit is the premium and the loss is potentially unlimited.
  4. OTC derivatives are privately negotiated and tailor-made but carry higher counterparty and liquidity risk.

Q3. Which statement best describes “Complaint handling”?

  1. STT is charged on derivatives transactions, for example on the sale of options on the premium and on futures sale, at rates set by law.

  3. On the expiry date the futures price converges to the spot price of the underlying.
  4. Intermediaries must have a grievance mechanism and resolve client complaints promptly, and escalate to the exchange or SEBI if not resolved.
