Key terms, examples and exam traps

Financial planning

Financial planning is a process of meeting life goals through the proper management of finances.

Example. A plan links goals, cash flow, risk and investments.

Exam trap. This statement is false: It is the same as selling products.

Six-step process

The steps are establishing the relationship, gathering data, analysing, recommending, implementing and monitoring.

Example. The adviser reviews the plan after it is implemented.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Monitoring is optional once the plan starts.

SEBI Investment Advisers Regulations

Anyone giving investment advice for a fee must be registered with SEBI under the 2013 regulations.

Example. A fee-charging adviser needs registration.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Anyone may give paid advice without registration.

Qualification and certification

Individual advisers need prescribed qualification and a NISM certification such as Series X-A and X-B, as the rules specify.

Example. An adviser passes the NISM certification.

Exam trap. This statement is false: No certification is required.

Fee structure

Advisers may charge a fixed fee or a fee linked to assets under advice, within a cap on fees from an individual or family per year, currently ₹1.51 lakh.

Example. A client pays a fixed annual fee.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Advisers may take commission from products they recommend.

Segregation

Advisory and distribution activities must be separated, and advisers cannot receive distribution commission from clients they advise.

Example. An adviser recommends direct plans.

Exam trap. This statement is false: An adviser may earn commissions from the same client.

Risk profiling

Risk profiling assesses a client's willingness, ability and need to take risk before recommending investments.

Example. A questionnaire and cash-flow review feed the profile.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Risk profiling is based on age alone.

Risk tolerance and risk capacity

Tolerance is the willingness to bear risk, and capacity is the financial ability to bear it; the lower one governs.

Example. A willing client with little savings gets a cautious plan.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Tolerance always determines the plan.

Suitability

Advice must be suitable to the client's profile, and the adviser must document the basis for recommendations.

Example. A written rationale accompanies each recommendation.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Suitability is checked once a decade.

Conflict of interest

Advisers must disclose conflicts of interest and manage them in the client's favour.

Example. An adviser discloses a relationship with a provider.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Conflicts need not be disclosed.

Risk profile report and agreement

Advisers must keep the signed agreement, risk profile and advice records for the prescribed period.

Example. The adviser retains records for the period required.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Records are optional.

Goal-based planning

Goals are listed with amount, time horizon and priority, inflation-adjusted and matched with suitable investments.

Example. A child's education in 12 years is funded by equity-oriented funds.

Exam trap. This statement is false: All goals are funded with the same asset.

Asset allocation

Asset allocation divides a portfolio among asset classes according to goals, horizon and risk, and drives most of the variation in returns.

Example. A balanced client holds equity and debt.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Asset allocation is about picking one winning stock.

Strategic and tactical allocation

Strategic allocation sets long-term target weights while tactical allocation makes short-term deviations to exploit views.

Example. A client's long-run mix is 60:40.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Tactical allocation is a fixed rule.

Rebalancing

Rebalancing returns the portfolio to target weights after market moves, by calendar or by threshold.

Example. A portfolio is reset when equity drifts above its band.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Rebalancing means adding only to winners.

Emergency fund

The emergency fund of three to six months of expenses is kept in liquid, safe instruments.

Example. Funds are kept in a liquid fund.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The emergency fund should be in small-cap equity.

Cash flow statement

A cash flow statement lists inflows and outflows in a period to find the surplus available for goals.

Example. A planner finds a monthly surplus.

Exam trap. This statement is false: It shows the client's net worth.

Net worth statement

Net worth is the difference between assets and liabilities at a date.

Example. Assets of ₹1 crore less loans of ₹30 lakh give ₹70 lakh.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Net worth is annual income minus expenses.

Debt ratios

A common guide is that EMIs should not exceed about 40% of income and total debt service should be sustainable.

Example. A client with EMIs of 55% of income is over-leveraged.

Exam trap. This statement is false: There is no useful guide on borrowing.

Savings ratio

The savings ratio is savings as a share of income and shows the client's capacity to fund goals.

Example. A saver putting away 25% of income.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Savings ratio is expenses divided by income.

Liquidity ratio

The liquidity ratio is liquid assets divided by monthly expenses and shows how many months the client can cope without income.

Example. Liquid assets of ₹3 lakh with expenses of ₹50,000 give 6 months.

Exam trap. This statement is false: It measures the share of equity in the portfolio.

Time value of money

A rupee today is worth more than a rupee in the future because it can earn a return.

Example. Planning uses present and future values.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Money has the same value at all times.

Future value

Future value is the amount an investment grows to at a given rate over a period.

Example. ₹1 lakh at 10% for 2 years becomes ₹1.21 lakh.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Future value is calculated by dividing by growth.

Present value

Present value discounts a future amount to today's equivalent at a given rate.

Example. ₹1.21 lakh in 2 years at 10% is worth ₹1 lakh today.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Present value is greater than future value for positive rates.

SIP for a goal

The monthly investment required for a goal is found from the future value of an annuity, given the expected return and time.

Example. A planner sets the SIP for a target corpus.

Exam trap. This statement is false: A SIP amount does not depend on time horizon.

CAGR

The compound annual growth rate expresses the annualised growth between two values over a period.

Example. ₹100 growing to ₹200 in 5 years is about 14.9% a year.

Exam trap. This statement is false: CAGR is the simple average of yearly returns.

Time-weighted return

Time-weighted return removes the effect of cash flows to measure the manager's performance.

Example. A fund's return is compared over a period fairly.

Exam trap. This statement is false: It depends on the timing of the client's deposits.

Sharpe ratio

The Sharpe ratio is excess return over the risk-free rate divided by standard deviation and measures return per unit of total risk.

Example. A higher Sharpe ratio is better for equal risk.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The Sharpe ratio uses beta as the risk measure.

CAPM

CAPM gives the required return as the risk-free rate plus beta times the market risk premium.

Example. Risk-free 7%, market premium 6%, beta 1.2 gives 14.2%.

Exam trap. This statement is false: CAPM uses standard deviation as the risk.

Diversification

Spreading investments across assets with low correlation reduces risk without proportionally lowering return.

Example. A portfolio holds equity, debt and gold.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Diversification removes market risk entirely.

Mutual fund selection

Selection looks at risk-adjusted returns, consistency, cost, portfolio quality and the manager, and prefers direct plans for advisers.

Example. An adviser compares expense ratios and Sharpe ratios.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Last year's top return is the only criterion.

Direct versus regular plans

Direct plans have lower expense ratios because no distributor commission is paid, which matters for fee-only advisers.

Example. A client's returns improve by choosing direct plans.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Direct plans carry higher costs.

Client communication

Communication should be clear, in the client's language, honest about risks and avoiding jargon and promises.

Example. An adviser explains the plan's assumptions.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Guaranteeing returns builds trust.

Plan monitoring

The plan is reviewed at least annually and when life events, markets or goals change.

Example. A marriage triggers a review.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Reviews are only needed on request.

Insurance and debt in the plan

A good plan first secures protection and removes costly debt before building long-term wealth.

Example. A client repays a high-interest loan before investing more.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Costly debt should be kept while investing.

Special situations

Advisers adapt plans for business owners, senior citizens, NRIs and clients with irregular income.

Example. A seasonal earner holds a larger buffer.

Exam trap. This statement is false: One standard plan fits every client.

Ethics and fiduciary duty

The adviser acts in the client's best interest, with integrity, confidentiality and care.

Example. An adviser puts the client's interest above her own.

Exam trap. This statement is false: The adviser's duty is to maximise her fee.

Grievance redressal

Clients can complain to the adviser, then SEBI through its portal, and the adviser must act on complaints promptly.

Example. A client files on SCORES.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Advisers are not subject to complaints.

Cases: reading the facts

In case questions, extract the client's goals, income, assets, liabilities, risk profile and assumptions into a table before answering.

Example. A candidate tabulates the case before solving.

Exam trap. This statement is false: Case answers can be guessed without the data.
